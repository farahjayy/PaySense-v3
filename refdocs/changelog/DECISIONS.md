# Architectural Decisions — PaySense v3

> Record decisions here so they aren't re-debated in future sessions.
> Format: **Decision** → **Why** → **Trade-offs / what was rejected**.
> Canonical one-line list lives in `refdocs/paysense-v3-PRD.md` §7; this file expands the load-bearing ones under the same id.

An ADR is worth writing when the decision (a) is expensive to reverse, (b) will look arbitrary to a future reader, or (c) had a real alternative someone will propose again. Everything else is just a commit message.

---

## D-01 — New public repo; v1 frozen; port proven modules with their tests
*2026-10-08 · author decision (scaffold interview)*

**Decision.** PaySense v2 lives in its own public repo. v1 (`../PaySense/`) is a read-only reference. Domain modules that are correct and tested in v1 (schedule, providers, ledger, plans, bills, categorize, CSV import, the Engine 2 CV harness, the frontend UI kit) are ported together with their tests; the engines, seed script and model artefacts are not.
**Why.** v1 was the experiment and accumulated 19 concept-document revisions of patches. Its domain code survived real use; its engines did not (see `context/v1-lessons.md`).
**Rejected.** Evolving v1 in place (drags the single-user schema, the personal-data history and the patched engines along); rewriting everything from scratch (re-learns solved problems such as sen rounding and provider start dates).

## D-03 — Username-only login, user-scoped repository layer
*2026-10-08 · supervisor decision, implementation by Claude*

**Decision.** `POST /api/auth/login {username}` looks up a pre-seeded user and returns a signed HS256 bearer token (secret from `SESSION_SECRET`, expiry 12 h). The frontend keeps it in `sessionStorage` and sends `Authorization: Bearer`. Every repository function takes `user_id` as its first argument; no query exists without it. The backend alone talks to Supabase (service key); RLS is enabled on every table with no policies for `anon`/`authenticated`, so a leaked anon key reads nothing.
**Why.** The supervisor ruled passwords and sign-up out of scope; each tester still needs isolated data. A bearer header avoids third-party-cookie problems between a Vercel origin and a Render origin.
**Trade-off.** Anyone who knows a username can log in as that user. Acceptable for a 5–7-person UAT on synthetic persona data; stated as a limitation and in the model card. Rejected: Supabase Auth (needs email/password or OAuth — out of scope), a profile picker with no login (supervisor asked for a username entry).

## D-04 — Engine 1 is a component forecaster with Monte Carlo uncertainty
*2026-10-08 · approved by author*

**Decision.** Known flows (confirmed recurring income and bills, BNPL instalments, dated savings goals) are placed on their exact dates. Only weekly variable series are forecast — discretionary spend, and (D-27) income not covered by a confirmed recurring item. A residual-bootstrap Monte Carlo (1,000 paths) produces P10/P50/P90 margins and P(shortfall). Everything is anchored to today. *(D-27 replaced the original daily balance with a monthly margin and removed the starting balance; the original text said "daily resolution, day-of-week spreading" and P10/P50/P90 balances.)*
**Why.** v1 forecast monthly *net* cash flow as one series: it decayed to zero, back-derived income bars from the net, collapsed to a flat line after one one-off entry, ignored stale logs, and lost to naive baselines. Banks split fixed and variable flows in production (BBVA, Monzo, Yodlee) and alert on P(negative balance) (BBVA, Intuit Mint ODEWS) — research §A.
**Rejected.** Daily discretionary forecasting (too noisy for a few months of data); per-category forecasting (too few points per category; possible future work); conformal intervals (residual bootstrap gives full paths, which the daily balance and P(shortfall) need).

## D-05 — Candidate algorithms stay within the literature review
*2026-10-08 · author asked for a recommendation; Claude recommended, author's earlier "full v2" choice of Chronos + EBM superseded*

**Decision.**
- Financial health forecasting candidates: **ARIMA, Prophet, LSTM** — plus **naive** and **moving-average** benchmarks. The LSTM is trained **globally** across many series (Berka / MoneyData / synthetic cohort) and applied per user.
- BNPL risk classification candidates: **Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM**, each with **monotonic constraints** — plus a **logistic-regression** benchmark.
- XAI: **SHAP**, plus counterfactual search.
**Why.** The literature review (supervisor-reviewed) selected these candidates; the methodology must follow from it, and Chapter 2 should not need new sections. Benchmarks are standard evaluation practice (fpp3; M4/M5), monotonic constraints are a model *configuration* (one methodology citation), and a global LSTM is how recurrent networks are normally applied to many short series. v1's failures were in design (target, label, features, evaluation), not in the choice of algorithms.
**Rejected (recorded as future work in the sources doc):** ETS/Theta, Chronos-2 / TimesFM / Moirai foundation models, Explainable Boosting Machines, WoE scorecards. Each would need new literature to justify.

## D-06 — Engine 1 selection rule
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision*

**Decision.** Rolling-origin evaluation on the weekly discretionary series (minimum training 26 weeks, step 4 weeks, horizons 4/8/13). Primary metric: MASE pooled across series. Also RMSSE, 80% interval coverage, end-of-horizon balance MAE. Significance: Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold-corrected Diebold–Mariano on pooled losses, Wilcoxon on per-series MASE, Friedman/Nemenyi across all five. Objective 1's "best model" = the best of ARIMA / Prophet / LSTM. The **served** model is that model unless it is significantly worse than the best benchmark; then the benchmark is served and the finding is reported.
**Why.** v1 selected on RMSE from one split without baselines, then lost to naive in production. MAPE is undefined near zero.
**Trade-off.** If a benchmark wins, the dissertation must say so plainly; the system's value then rests on decomposition and simulation, which is still a defensible contribution.

## D-07 — Engine 2's target is Engine 1's simulated shortfall probability
*2026-10-08 · approved by author*

**Decision.** For each synthetic profile with a proposed purchase, run the served Engine 1 with the proposed plan added and take P(cumulative margin < RM0 at any month-end within the horizon) as the target. *(Originally "balance < RM0 after any instalment due date"; changed by D-27.)*
**Why.** v1's label was a hand-written formula the model simply re-learned (oracle F1 0.836, every model within 0.02), and the Engine 1 feature carried ≈1.5% of label variance. A simulation-derived label (a) has published precedent — Bijak, Thomas & Mues' dynamic affordability assessment; (b) mirrors BNM's financial-margin stress test; (c) matches the CCA 2025 affordability standard ("repay … without undue financial hardship over the duration"); (d) answers the supervisor's demand to justify how the label is assigned; and (e) makes the forecast genuinely load-bearing.
**Rejected.** Expert-rule labels and weak supervision (re-encode the author's opinion), transfer from public default datasets (wrong population, no schedule features — used only as a directional sanity check).
**Open.** Recommend the author shows this design to the supervisor / faculty consultants before P5 (PRD §8 Q7).

## D-08 — One synthetic population generator
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision*

**Decision.** `ml/datagen` generates transaction-level student histories from one cited parameter file (`ml/datagen/params.yaml`). Sources: Tan et al. 2026 and Osman et al. 2024 (demographics, income brackets), PTPTN 2026 rates (lump-sum disbursements), Tok & Cheah 2024 (monthly spend), DOSM HES 2022 (category shares), BNM / CCOB repayment statistics (~88% always on time, ~12% late-but-paid, < 0.5% unable to pay), provider fee schedules. The same generator produces the Engine 2 training profiles, the Engine 1 synthetic cohort and the three UAT persona CSVs.
**Why.** One set of assumptions to justify and document; the persona CSVs are statistically the same population the models learned from.
**Trade-off.** Parameters without a source are marked `assumed` in the file and listed in the dissertation's limitations.

## D-09 — One feature module, enforced by a schema hash
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision*

**Decision.** `backend/app/engines/risk/features.py` is the only place Engine 2 features are computed. `ml/engine2_train` imports it; it never re-implements a feature. Each artefact's `metadata.json` stores the ordered feature list and its hash; the server refuses to start on a mismatch.
*Amended 2026-10-09: the hash also covers the windows and caps that change feature values without changing a name (`RECENT_MONTHS`, `COMMITMENT_MONTHS`, the 30-day commitment month, `RATIO_CAP`, `SEN_PER_RM`), which now live in `features.py`.*
**Why.** v1 trained `bnpl_income_ratio` as total debt/income and served it as monthly burden/income, and `forecasted_cash_flow` meant different things in training and serving.

## D-10 — Engine 2 training & selection protocol
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision*

**Decision.** Soft-label training by duplicate-and-weight; 5×5 repeated CV stratified by target decile and grouped by profile; Optuna tuning in an inner loop with the budget written to metadata; Brier primary, log-loss / ROC-AUC (p ≥ 0.30) / calibration slope / ECE secondary; Nadeau–Bengio corrected t-tests; calibration (none / Platt / isotonic) by nested CV; ablation without Engine-1 features; stress scenarios (income −20%, delayed PTPTN).
**Why.** Ports v1's proven harness and fixes its gaps (untuned defaults, single split, unreported tuning).
*Amended 2026-10-09 by D-37 (lockbox, nested ablation, stored calibrator); the stratification is on quantile bins of the label, at most ten, not exactly deciles, because labels tie at 0 and 1.*


## D-11 — Repayment history is a flag, not a feature
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision*

**Decision.** Overdue instalments (unpaid and past due, whatever their age) and instalments paid late in the last 6 months are shown as a separate, rule-based flag citing BNM's suspension after 3 consecutive missed payments; only unpaid instalments count as missed, so late payments alone never reach the warning level. They are not a model feature.
**Why.** The simulation label is about cash flow; a synthetic missed-payment variable would have to be wired into it by hand, recreating v1's distortion (synthetic users averaged ~3 misses, so "0 missed" dominated). Regulators require repayment history to be *checked*, which the flag does visibly.
**Trade-off.** The model score alone ignores repayment behaviour; the UI always shows both.

## D-15 — Hosting with a measured escape hatch
*2026-10-08 · supervisor plan + Claude fallback; step 1 triggered by the P0 spike*

**Decision.** Vercel (frontend), Render (backend), Supabase Singapore (DB). The P0 spike measured a process with the full stack including PyTorch at **607 MB RSS** (> Render's 512 MB); the served set without torch is ~300–470 MB. So step 1 of the escape hatch is **adopted now**: the backend runtime never imports torch; the global LSTM is trained offline (torch lives in the `ml` dependency group) and served via **ONNX Runtime**. If the real served stack (re-measured at the end of P4 and P6, then on Render in P8) still exceeds ~450 MB, move the backend to Google Cloud Run at 1 GiB.
**Why.** Render free is 512 MB / 0.1 CPU and sleeps after 15 minutes; torch alone adds ~170 MB.
**Trade-off.** Exporting the LSTM to ONNX adds a train→export→parity-check step (P3/P4) and a test that the ONNX output matches torch.

## D-19 — Running-ledger balance by default — **SUPERSEDED by D-27**
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision; superseded the same day when the author asked for a flow-based design. Kept for the record; do not implement.*

**Decision.** Every transaction create/edit/delete and every instalment marked paid moves the balance; balance sync records a correction. The persona onboarding sets an opening balance.
**Why.** v1's manually synced balance went stale and its ledger mode was never switched on; Engine 1's simulation starts from this number.

## D-23 — Python version pinned by uv
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision; verified by the P0 spike (`reports/spikes/p0-dependency-spike.md`)*

**Decision.** Use `uv` with `.python-version` = **3.12**. The spike installed and imported the full stack on 3.12.15: FastAPI, supabase, statsforecast, prophet, torch (CPU index), onnxruntime, scikit-learn, xgboost, lightgbm, shap, optuna, scipy, statsmodels. `requires-python` is bounded to `>=3.12,<3.13`; torch is installed from the PyTorch CPU index with `--index-strategy unsafe-best-match`.

## D-24 — Three named providers; everything else is manual "Other"
*2026-10-08 · author decision; revised same day after the author asked whether to include Grab and Boost (recommendation: exclude)*

**Decision.** The provider list is **SPayLater, TikTok PayLater, Atome and "Other provider (manual)"**.
- SPayLater, TikTok PayLater and Atome are rule-driven. Their rules come from real student users' checkouts: SPayLater and TikTok PayLater charge nothing at checkout and send the first bill one month after the purchase; Atome charges the first payment on the purchase date. The schedule auto-fills and the first date stays editable.
- "Other provider" applies **no rule and no fee formula**. The user types a provider name, the first payment date, the number of instalments and either the instalment amount or the total payable shown at their checkout. The app splits the total exactly in sen (leftover sen in the last instalment) and builds the schedule from exactly what was entered. The plan carries a small "schedule uses the dates and amounts you entered" note.
- Grab PayLater and Boost PayFlex are **not named** in the app. A provider becomes a named, rule-driven option only after a real checkout example is recorded in `refdocs/context/bnpl-billing-rules-my.md` and a test pins its schedule.

**Why.** The author has first-hand information only for these three. A guessed first due date creates phantom missed payments and moves instalments into the wrong weeks of the simulation, which is Engine 2's label (D-07). Naming a provider implies the app understands it, even with an "unverified" badge. Their fees also don't fit the single monthly-rate formula (research §C, medium confidence: Boost charges a flat RM5–10 fee plus 2.5% per month; Grab's interest varies by term), so a typed checkout amount is more accurate than any formula the app could apply.
**Rejected.** Listing Grab/Boost as "unverified" providers (the earlier version of this ADR: still implies support, and still needs a fee formula); encoding their rules from secondary web sources; hiding the "Other" option (students use other providers, and the stack view must include every plan they have).

## D-25 — Berka is a benchmark, not the population
*2026-10-08 · author approved its use*

**Decision.** The Berka / PKDD'99 Czech bank dataset is used only (a) as a real multi-account benchmark for comparing Engine 1's forecasters — its payment-type codes give ground truth for the fixed/variable split — and (b) as extra series for pre-training the global LSTM. It is never used to describe or represent the target users.
**Why.** PaySense targets Malaysian university students with a bank/debit account and e-wallets and usually no credit card. Berka is real, account-level, transaction-level data — rare and valuable for evaluating forecasting — but its account holders are Czech adults in the 1990s. The student population is represented by the cited synthetic generator (D-08).
**Trade-off.** Engine 1 results on Berka show how the methods behave on real household series, not on students; the dissertation reports them separately from the synthetic-student cohort.

## D-26 — Money stored as bigint sen; cross-user links blocked by composite foreign keys
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision (P0, migration 0001)*

**Decision.** Every money column is `bigint` integer sen (`amount_sen`, `current_balance_sen`, `total_price_sen`, `limit_sen`) — v1's `numeric(12,2)` is not carried over. Every table except `users` has `user_id`; every link between user-owned tables is a composite `(child_id, user_id) → parent (id, user_id)` foreign key (`unique (id, user_id)` on the parents), with `ON DELETE SET NULL (col)` (Postgres 15+) where a link is optional. Provider keys: `spaylater`, `tiktok_paylater`, `atome`, `other`, with a typed `provider_label` and a `schedule_source` of `provider_rule` or `manual` (D-24). `transactions.source` drops v1's `pdf` (out of scope).
**Why.** supabase-py returns `numeric` as a JSON float, which would reintroduce rounding in code that D-16 requires to be integer; storing sen keeps the whole stack integer from disk to engine. The composite keys make "a transaction pointing at someone else's plan" impossible at the database level, not just untested in the repository — defence in depth for hard constraint 4.
**Trade-off.** The API boundary must convert sen ↔ RM (decided in P1); hand-written SQL in the dashboard shows sen, not ringgit.

## D-27 — Engine 1 forecasts flows, not a balance
*2026-10-08 · author direction ("predict just by using the income and expenses prediction"); Claude specified the details. Supersedes D-19 and the balance anchor in D-04 / D-07 / D-21.*

**Decision.** There is no starting balance, no running ledger and no balance sync (`user_settings` no longer has balance columns). For each month of the horizon, each Monte Carlo path computes `margin = income − bills − BNPL instalments (incl. any checkout payment) − discretionary spend`. Confirmed recurring income, bills and instalments are *placed*; discretionary spend and any income not covered by a confirmed recurring item are *forecast* as weekly series by the same served model and residual bootstrap. Cumulative margin starts at RM0 today ("no savings assumed"). **Shortfall** = cumulative margin < RM0 at any month-end in the horizon; **P(shortfall)** is the share of paths where that happens, and it is Engine 2's label (D-07). The 4-month history gate stays (supervisor). Incomplete-looking months are warned about and kept (their noise widens the bands); they are never hidden behind a fake-precise number. The balance chart becomes a **margin chart** (monthly margin and cumulative margin bands, with and without the plan).
**Why.** Most tracking apps — and most students — have no reliable account balance, so a balance anchor (D-19) depended on the user logging every transaction; any gap silently corrupted every forecast and every Engine 2 label. BNM's own household stress test uses a *financial margin* (income − necessities − debt obligations), not a balance, so the flow definition is as citable as the balance one (research §B2). The author's three reference cases come out right without a balance: RM600 over 3 months is Safe on RM2,000 monthly income, At Risk on RM500 monthly income, and At Risk when living on RM2,000 savings with no income (the plan drains it).
**Consequences.** (1) Engine 2 features `min_balance_p10_without` and `balance_to_monthly_essentials` are replaced by margin- and income-based ones (PRD §3.2); `income_to_due_gap_days` is dropped because the label no longer depends on intra-month timing — timing is shown as a note. (2) P3 must evaluate the forecasters on a second series (weekly unconfirmed income) as well as discretionary spend. (3) The dashboard shows a month summary (income / expenses / net), not a balance. (4) Migration 0001 was edited before it was applied: the balance columns were removed.
**Trade-off / limitation.** Savings are not credited, so a student with real savings and no income looks At Risk; the UI says "this check assumes you pay from your monthly income, not savings". An optional "savings buffer" input is recorded as future work, not built. Intra-month timing (an instalment due days before the next income) is no longer part of the label.
**Rejected.** Keeping the running-ledger balance with stronger warnings (fragile to missing entries, the author's concern); an optional dated balance checkpoint with gap simulation (more machinery, and the author preferred flows); a hard completeness gate (testers with messy data would be stuck).

## D-28 — Provider limit caps the financed amount; the excess is paid at checkout
*2026-10-08 · author-described real checkout behaviour; details assumed (see ASSUMED 5)*

**Decision.** A provider's remaining limit = recorded limit − what is still unpaid on that provider's active plans. For a purchase of price P: `financed = min(P, remaining limit)`, `checkout payment = P − financed` (an immediate outflow in the purchase month, on top of Atome's first-instalment-at-checkout rule). Any monthly fee applies to the financed part only. If the provider carries a fee, the cap applies to the financed **total payable including the fee**, so the financed principal is the largest amount whose principal plus fee fits the remaining limit. No limit recorded = no cap, and the report says the limit was unknown. Engine 2 gets `upfront_to_income`, `limit_utilisation_with_purchase` and `limit_known` (PRD §3.2); the BNPL stack view shows total limit and utilisation across providers. Worked example from the author: limit RM200, price RM300 → RM100 at checkout, RM200 financed.
**Why.** Real providers do not let a purchase exceed the limit, they require the balance up front; ignoring that either rejects feasible purchases or understates the cash needed this month.
**Not modelled.** Using spare limit as a cash-flow buffer or credit-card substitute (strategic use of a large limit). That is a behavioural assumption the literature review does not support; the headroom is displayed and counted in utilisation only. Recorded as future work.
**Open.** Whether real providers cap on principal or on principal plus fee, and how they count used limit, is `ASSUMED:` until the author supplies a real SPayLater / TikTok / Atome example.

## D-29 — API money is integer sen; refunds/transfers are reserved categories; import is stateless
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision (P1 plan)*

**Decision.**
1. **Money over the wire is integer sen** (`*_sen` fields), never floating-point RM and never decimal strings. Request fields use a strict integer type, so `100.5`, `"100"` and `true` are rejected; a test fails if any OpenAPI property named `*_sen` is not an integer or any money-looking property is a float. D-16's "convert at the API boundary" is read as "convert at the *presentation* boundary": the frontend formats sen as `RM 1,234.56` and parses what a person types from the text (`parse_rm` / a `lib/money.ts` twin), so no float ever touches a ringgit amount.
2. **Refunds and own-account transfers use two reserved categories**, `Refund` and `Transfer` — no schema change. A refund is stored as income but never counts as income: it nets against spend in its month. A transfer is ignored in both directions. `domain/flows.classify` is the only place this is decided; Engine 1 (P4) imports it. `Transfer` keywords are limited to explicit own-account wording because a bare "transfer in" on a statement is usually an allowance.
3. **CSV import is stateless.** Preview returns rows; confirm receives the user's edited rows and re-validates them. Nothing is held server-side between the two calls. Rows that match already-stored transactions (same day, amount, direction, normalised description; multiplicity-aware) are flagged as duplicates and unchecked by default.
**Why.** v1 stored RM floats and converted at the edges; rounding bugs there are what the sen discipline exists to prevent. Refunds netting against spend keeps the discretionary series honest; counting them as income would inflate the income forecast. Render restarts and sleeps, so in-memory preview state would lose uploads.
**Rejected.** RM floats (v1); RM decimal strings (every client must parse them, no benefit over sen); negative transaction amounts (the `amount_sen > 0` invariant is load-bearing); a new transaction column for refunds (not needed yet); a server-side preview store.

## D-30 — A payment is tied to its instalment; ambiguous failures never delete a payment
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision after the P1 FastAPI review (HIGH finding)*

**Decision.** `transactions.bnpl_installment_id` links a logged payment to the instalment it settles, with a **partial unique index** (one payment per instalment) and a composite `(id, user_id)` foreign key. Logging a payment is therefore idempotent (`_ensure_payment` finds the existing row or inserts it). `mark_paid` logs first, then flips the instalment with a conditional update; if the flip errors it **re-reads the instalment** and treats a committed flip as success — it never deletes the payment on an ambiguous error. `create_plan` inserts instalments as unpaid, logs the checkout payments, and only then flips the checkout instalments to paid; on failure it deletes everything linked to the plan, each cleanup step independent so a failed step cannot mask the original error or skip the plan delete. `delete_many` sends ids in batches of 100.
**Why.** supabase-py has no transactions, and a client timeout looks identical whether or not Postgres committed. The first design ("on any error, delete the payment I logged") could erase a real payment after a committed-but-unacknowledged flip, leaving a paid instalment with no expense and no way to repair it (a retry says "already paid"). Tying the payment to the instalment makes the worst case recoverable (a logged payment against a still-unpaid instalment, fixed by one retry) instead of silent.
**Trade-off.** A transient inconsistency window remains (payment logged, instalment not yet flipped); both the forecast and the month summary would briefly count the cash as spent while the instalment still shows as due. **Rejected for now:** moving create-plan / mark-paid into Postgres functions via `supabase.rpc` for true transactions — the cleaner fix, but it moves business logic into SQL and splits it from the tested Python; revisit if the real-database tests show the window matters.

## D-31 — Synthetic population: what the generator may and may not show the models
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision after the P2 python and ML audits*

**Decision.**
1. **Answer keys are separated from inputs.** Each exported profile nests the persona, the generator's true income, the true provider limits and the recurring ground truth under `truth`; the Engine 1 cohort has no persona column. Training and serving code must never read `truth` (a P5 test enforces it); evaluation code may.
2. **The proposed purchase is built against the limit the student has *recorded*, not the true limit** (D-28: no recorded limit means no cap). Existing plans in the history are built against the true limit, because the provider really enforced it.
3. **Repayment behaviour is drawn once per student.** The cited 88 / 12 / <0.5% are shares of account holders (CCOB), so a student is "always on time", "sometimes late" or "misses one"; they are not per-instalment probabilities.
4. **The weekly series carries structure the forecasters are not told about** (AR(1) drift in the spending level, a payday-week lift, exam dips, outlier weeks). Day-of-week and payday-day effects only move spending within a week, so they are *not* claimed as weekly-scale difficulty.
5. **Every parameter is in `params.yaml`** with a citation or an `assumed` reason; structural constants that change outputs are parameters too. The report separates distribution matches from "wiring checks" that hold by construction.
6. *(2026-10-09 note)* **Engine 2 labels draw each profile's as-of day** across a full year (`calendar.as_of_window_start` / `calendar.as_of_window_days`, its own random stream), so the PTPTN position, the semester and the horizon differ between labels. The first label run gave every profile the same day (2025-12-28), which made the calendar identical for all 10,000 labels. The Engine 1 cohort and the persona files keep the fixed day, so P3's results are unaffected.
**Why.** The first version leaked the true limit into the purchase (train/serve skew on `limit_known`), applied a holder-level statistic to instalments (only 37% of holders were fully on time against a cited 88%), and claimed weekly-scale richness it did not have, so a "forecaster beats benchmarks" result on it would have been partly an artefact. Each was found by the ml-auditor and fixed before any model saw the data.
**Trade-off.** More assumed parameters (105 of 121) and a noisier weekly total than the drawn one; the report says both. The synthetic population remains a stand-in, never evidence about real students.

## D-33 — The cumulative margin starts at the beginning of the current month, not at "today"
*2026-10-08 · Claude decision while building the P4 simulation (amends the wording of D-27)*

**Decision.** D-27 said the cumulative margin starts at RM0 "today". Read literally, a student paid on the 1st and checked on the 12th would have that income ignored (it is in the past) while the rest of the month's rent and spending is counted, so almost everyone looks At Risk mid-month. The cumulative margin now starts at RM0 at the **start of the current month** ("no savings carried in" is unchanged) and the current month's margin is the **actual net so far this month** (income minus spend, refunds netted, savings excluded; transactions dated today are left to the forecast) plus the simulated remainder. Months before the current one do not enter.
**Why.** It keeps D-27's principle (no balance, no savings credited, flows only) while making the answer independent of which day of the month the user looks. Found by the first service test: a comfortable, correctly confirmed ledger came out At Risk on the 12th.
**Trade-off.** Savings from earlier months still do not count (D-27 limitation), and a user who has already spent heavily this month starts the month behind, which is the honest reading. Engine 2's labels use the same function, so training and serving agree.

## D-34 — Engine 1 serves the global LSTM, exported to ONNX
*2026-10-09 · decided mechanically by the D-06 rule from the P3 evaluation (`reports/engine1/`)*

**Decision.** The forecaster behind Engine 1 is the **global LSTM**: one network per series kind (weekly discretionary spend, weekly income), a 26-week input window scaled by its own mean, a 13-week output, 32 hidden units, L1 loss, trained offline on the synthetic cohort and **served through ONNX Runtime**; torch never enters the API process (D-15). Beyond 13 weeks the forecast is continued recursively (an extension that was not itself evaluated).
**Evidence.** Rolling-origin evaluation of Naive, 4- and 13-week moving averages, AutoARIMA, Prophet and the LSTM on 60 synthetic profiles per kind (three history lengths), MoneyData (one real UK account) and 60 Berka accounts: geometric-mean MASE at 13 weeks 0.815 for the LSTM against 1.044 for the best benchmark (13-week moving average) and 1.046 for AutoARIMA; median per-series difference -0.110 (95% CI -0.150 to -0.078), Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon p = 2e-37 over 242 series; the LSTM is also the best model on each dataset separately and at each history length (17, 26, 52 weeks). On 13-week cumulative-margin error it ranks first overall, but AutoARIMA is better on the synthetic cohort (2.13 against 2.90).
**Why this is not unconditional.** The LSTM under-forecasts spend (mean signed error about -0.27 naive-error units on synthetic spend, -0.94 on Berka spend), the synthetic series share one calendar and the generator is the author's own, and the LSTM was trained on synthetic data and applied to the real datasets without fine-tuning. The P4 residual bootstrap resamples the model's own signed past errors, which re-centres paths on the model's record; P4 measures that on the cohort and reports it (`reports/engine1/serving_check.md`).
**Cost.** About 1 ms per origin against 250-600 ms for Prophet and AutoARIMA, which is what makes a per-request error record affordable.
**Rejected.** Serving AutoARIMA (better on synthetic margin, but 350 ms per fit, ten fits per request) and the 13-week moving average (the strongest benchmark, 28% worse than the LSTM on MASE).

## D-35 — The forecast gate, the incomplete-month rules and the actual / forecast boundary
*2026-10-09 · Claude decision during P4 (resolves ASSUMED 6 for thresholds; thresholds themselves remain assumptions)*

**Decision.**
1. **Gate.** A forecast is produced only with at least four calendar months containing a real transaction up to today (supervisor constraint) **and** at least 17 complete Monday-Sunday weeks of usable history (a user with four calendar months touched but eight weeks of data would otherwise get a falsely precise answer). Otherwise the response is `locked` with a reason (`needs_four_months` or `no_usable_history`), never an error.
2. **Incomplete months** are listed, not hidden: a month with nothing logged (`no_data`); a month with no income when income appears in at least half of the other complete months and at least two of them (`no_income`; a semester-funded student is not told most months are incomplete); a month whose spending is under 40% of the median month (`low_spend`). The current month is never flagged. The forecast is still produced; the noisy month stays in the error record so the bands widen.
3. **Actual versus forecast.** Everything before `forecast_from`, the Monday after the last complete *logged* week, is actual (the month-to-date net comes from the ledger, D-33); everything from `forecast_from` on is rule and model: known flows placed on their dates and the forecast variable series. A ledger that stopped being updated is therefore forecast from where it stopped (with a staleness flag after 14 days), not read as weeks of zero spending. A confirmed recurring item carries the merchant text it matches (`match_text`), separate from its display name, and the forecast tells the user when a confirmed item matches nothing logged.
4. **Uncertainty.** The user's own error record is kept as whole eight-week paths from up to ten recent origins (not a pool of single weeks) and joined to cover the horizon; with fewer than two paths the series' own spread is used (`fallback`), never zero noise. Recorded errors are widened by a factor of 1.5 around their own mean (`ERROR_INFLATION`, an assumption): this narrows but does not close the gap to nominal coverage (`reports/engine1/serving_check.md`: about 0.5-0.7 inside P10-P90 against 0.8).
**Why.** The thresholds come from the audit scenarios, not from data: a stale ledger flipped a comfortable user from Safe to At Risk; four calendar months with eight weeks of data produced p = 0 and "Safe"; a deleted month was invisible; flat resampling of single weeks gave a third of the true spread over a long horizon.
**Trade-off.** All thresholds are assumptions with no real-user evidence; they are tested on synthetic ledgers with deliberately broken months and will be revisited after UAT. The forecast remains more confident than it should be on short histories.

## D-36 — A risk check simulates to the last instalment of the plan
*2026-10-09 · Claude decision after the P5 ml-auditor's first pass (resolves ASSUMED 2)*

**Decision.** The Monte Carlo horizon is a parameter of `simulate` (`max_weeks`, default 52, so the 3-month dashboard forecast behaves exactly as before). A risk check, and therefore every Engine 2 label and every served Engine 2 score, passes `RISK_CHECK_MAX_WEEKS = 180`: the longest plan the app allows (36 monthly instalments, about 157 weeks) plus a month to the first instalment and the rounding up to a whole month. The "without the plan" arm runs on the same horizon, so with and without are comparable. The cap remains as a bound and sets `horizon_capped` if it is ever hit; the Engine 2 report refuses to run when any label is truncated.
**Why.** With the 52-week cap, 3,834 of the first 10,000 labels were truncated (about 28% of a truncated plan's payable was never simulated), so the label understated the risk of long plans and the model could learn "more instalments is safer" from an artefact of the cap. Found by the ml-auditor before any full training run; the labels were regenerated (label version 2).
**Trade-off.** Beyond 13 weeks the served LSTM continues recursively (D-34), and that extension was never evaluated; a 36-instalment label depends on about 150 weeks of it, with the error record resampled in 8-week paths and widened by the fixed 1.5 factor (D-35). The long-plan labels are therefore the least trustworthy ones, and the report says so. **Rejected:** keeping the cap and flagging long plans with a note (the original ASSUMED 2: the label itself was biased, a note does not repair it); refusing plans over 12 months (the app allows them).

## D-40 — The risk model is served as ONNX; reasons are exact Shapley values against a reference student
*2026-10-09 · Claude decision (P6; supplements D-12)*

**Decision.** The P5 winner (LightGBM) is exported to ONNX (`ml/engine2_train/export.py`) and served with onnxruntime, which the API already needs; the calibrator is stored as plain numbers in `metadata.json`; there is no pickle and no training library in the API process. A result's reasons are exact **baseline Shapley values**: the model (calibration included) is run on every coalition of the features that differ from a stored reference student (the median, feature by feature, of the development profiles whose label is Safe: a typical student who is not at risk), in one batch of at most 2^16 rows, and each feature's average marginal effect is its contribution. Contributions add up to `p(student) - p(reference)` on the scale the student sees. A feature is shown as a reason only if it pushes the probability **up** by at least 0.02 (D-12).
**Why.** The API image has to stay small (D-15: about 100 MB measured without the ML stack), and the `shap` library plus a tree library would not. Tree SHAP is exact only on the model's raw log-odds, but the 0.02 threshold and every number a student sees are on the calibrated probability scale; enumerating coalitions is exact on that scale, works for any of the four D-05 families, and costs 91 ms for the full 65,536-row batch on the real model.
**Rejected.** The `shap` package at serving time (memory, import time, raw-scale contributions); scaling raw-scale SHAP values to the probability scale (an approximation with no guarantee); interventional SHAP over a background sample (K times the cost, no clearer to explain).
**Trade-off.** Contributions are relative to one reference student, not an average over the population, so a reason means "compared with a typical student who is not at risk"; a different reference would split the same total differently. The first export used the median of *all* development profiles, which is itself in the Caution band (about 0.21), so a Caution result had only a few hundredths of contribution to share out and often showed "nothing stands out" (found by the serving audit); the reference is now the median of the 3,505 Safe-labelled development profiles (a re-export, no retraining), and on 200 fresh students no non-Safe result is left without a reason. The reference is stored in the artefact and changes only with a new export. ONNX Runtime evaluates trees in float32, so on 1 of 3,000 check rows the served probability differs from the trained model by about 3e-4 (the rest by under 1e-6); the export records this in `metadata.json` and refuses an export that differs by more than 1e-3 (amended by D-55).
**Guards (closes the P5 audit's M4).** At start-up the loader refuses a model unless the feature schema hash, the feature order, the simulation constants that made the labels (`RISK_CHECK_MAX_WEEKS`, `DASHBOARD_MONTHS`, `ERROR_INFLATION`, `BOOTSTRAP_BLOCK`), the name of the served forecast model, the checksums of the exact forecast network files and of the forecast settings file (`models/engine1/v1/metadata.json`) that made the labels all equal what is running, and the model file matches its recorded checksum. A model folder without its metadata stops start-up instead of silently serving an older version. Not guarded (listed by the second serving audit): label path count, minimum error paths and the completeness thresholds.
**Two numbers on screen.** The shown probability and band are the model's; a direct simulation of the same purchase is shown beside it, with a plain sentence saying which decides the band and a notice when the two put the purchase in different bands (95.0% agree on 200 fresh students; the gap reaches 0.51).

## D-41 — The smallest change: a fixed grid, one change before two, a written effort order
*2026-10-09 · Claude decision (P6; implements D-12)*

**Decision.** For a result that is not Safe the check searches, in order, single changes then pairs of changes of different kinds, and returns the first that the model puts under 0.10: **more instalments** (only counts above the current one, from 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12), **buying after the next confirmed income** (the schedule moves by the same number of days), **a price 10%, 25% or 50% lower** (only when a price was entered), **another rule-driven provider** at the same price and count. Within a group the order is a stated effort (1 per step up the count list, 2 for waiting, 3 + percent/25 for a price cut, 6 for a provider); at most 60 candidates are tried. If none reaches Safe the best one found is returned and the screen says plainly that no single change gets there. Every candidate goes through the whole pipeline (plan rules, both simulations, features, calibrated model); the ledger is read once and the "without the purchase" simulation is reused for candidates that end on the same date. Candidates are screened at 300 simulated futures and any that screens Safe or within 0.02 of it, and the best one when none does, is re-run at 1,000 before it is shown.
**Refinements from the serving audit.** (1) The model has no feature that involves when a purchase happens, so it cannot rate "wait for your next income"; that lever is judged by the direct simulation of the exact plan, and the screen says so (`basis: simulation`). (2) A best effort that is no better than doing nothing is not offered. (3) Switching provider is only suggested to a provider whose limit the student recorded: with no record there is no cap and no utilisation, and the model would read missing information as good news. (4) The wait date is looked up at most 300 days ahead so that "Try this" always falls inside the date window the API accepts (31 days back to 366 ahead).
**Why.** An exact search over actions a student can take is explainable ("this is the smallest change we tried that brings it under 10%") and cannot recommend something the model does not itself rate as safe. A written ordering makes "smallest" defensible.
**Rejected.** Gradient or feature-space counterfactuals (they produce feature values no student can choose); searching for the lowest probability regardless of effort (it would always recommend not buying).
**Trade-off.** `ASSUMED:` the list of instalment counts a student can pick is a judgement (the providers' menus are not available to the app), and the effort weights are a convention; both are constants in `counterfactual.py`. On 200 fresh synthetic students, a change that reaches Safe was found for 11.9% of the results that were not Safe, and 90.5% of those students were already short of money before the purchase (a simulated chance of 10% or more without it), which no change to the purchase fixes; 13 of the 15 changes found are Safe by the direct simulation too (`reports/engine2/serving_check.md`, measured, not assumed).

## D-38 — Content-Security-Policy with a per-request script nonce
*2026-10-09 · Claude decision (deferred from the P1 reviews)*

**Decision.** The frontend sends a Content-Security-Policy built per request in `frontend/proxy.ts` (text in `lib/csp.ts`): scripts only by a fresh nonce plus `'strict-dynamic'` (no `'unsafe-inline'`, no `'unsafe-eval'` in production), `connect-src` limited to this origin and the API origin from `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_BASE_URL` (the build fails closed without it), no framing, plugins or foreign form posts. Every page is rendered per request (`await connection()` in the root layout) because Next can only stamp a nonce on a page rendered after the request. `style-src` keeps `'unsafe-inline'`.
**Why.** The bearer token lives in `sessionStorage` (D-03), so an injected script could read it; a script policy that blocks injected script and limits where a running script can send data closes the main route. Server-rendered `style=""` attributes (progress bars, sizing) cannot carry a nonce, and style injection is a far smaller risk, with images and fonts limited to this origin and `data:`/`blob:`.
**Rejected.** `'unsafe-inline'` scripts (no protection); experimental SRI hashes (static pages, but experimental in Next 16); a nonce with nonce-only styles (breaks the inline style attributes).
**Trade-off.** No static prerendering or CDN caching of pages: one server render per page view, acceptable for a per-user app of this size. Verified on the production build: header present, every script tag carries the request's nonce, 12 Playwright tests pass with no policy violations.

## D-39 — Per-user limits and short sessions for the study deployment

## D-37 — Engine 2 evaluation protocol: a lockbox, nested ablation, a stored calibrator
*2026-10-09 · Claude decision after the P5 ml-auditor's first pass (amends the protocol of D-10)*

**Decision.** (1) Every tenth profile (`index % 10 == 0`) is a **lockbox**: excluded from cross-validation, model choice, final tuning, calibration and the ablation. The final calibrated winner is fitted on the other 90% and scored once on the lockbox (Brier, reliability, band agreement), with the logistic benchmark and the constant beside it. (2) The ablation tunes **both** arms inside every training fold on the same folds (the full arm is the winner's own nested CV, reused); intervals use the t critical value for J-1 degrees of freedom. (3) Calibration is chosen and fitted on **single-repeat** out-of-fold predictions of the development set, then stored (`calibrator.json`) with the final model. (4) Results are reported for all profiles and separately for the profiles whose simulated shortfall chance without the purchase is under 0.10, where the plan decides the band. (5) Stress variants are scored on the lockbox only, with the stored calibrated model, against an `identity` run with the same path count and seed. (6) Stale and incomplete ledgers are used only in the stress test and never trained on (this settles a conflict between the plan, which said they join the training set, and the execution doc, which kept them apart; the stress test is a robustness evaluation, and training on them would contaminate it).
*Amended 2026-10-09 by the second ml-auditor pass, before the full run (nothing had been scored on the real lockbox):* (a) the ablation's full arm is no longer the winner's model-comparison run (it carries a winner's curse the without-arm lacks); both arms, and both arms of the monotonicity run, are computed fresh on the same folds with seed + 1000; (b) the lockbox constant of a subgroup is the subgroup's own development mean; (c) the low-baseline subgroup is also scored for band agreement and reliability, and a descriptive Brier breakdown by label class (exactly 0 / between / exactly 1) and by 0 < p_without < 1 is reported; (d) the lockbox is scored LAST, after every step that can fail, and each scoring is appended to `data/engine2/runs/lockbox_log.jsonl`, whose line count is the reported number of scorings; (e) the logistic coefficient signs are compared with the monotone directions.

**Why.** The first harness reported the winner on the folds that chose it, tuned the ablation's hyperparameters on all data (including the test folds), averaged five repeats' predictions before choosing a calibration the served single model does not have, and ran the stress variants on profiles that had shaped the model. Half the generated students were already short before the purchase, so a headline Brier mostly measured the baseline.
**Trade-off.** The lockbox is 10% of the profiles that no choice may use (the final model trains on the other 90%); a single lockbox score has sampling noise of its own and is not repeated. The PTPTN stress variant now removes the next disbursement instead of editing the ledger (see the stress module); it only changes students whose detected recurring items include a semester income.

## D-43 — Categories: twelve built in and locked, the person's own stored per user and manageable
*2026-10-15 · Claude decision (the author's walk-through; extends D-42)*

**Decision.** (1) The twelve categories the importer guesses from (Food, Transport, Rent, Salary, Allowance, Shopping, Bills, Entertainment, Education, Refund, Transfer, Other) plus `BNPL` (set by plans, never picked by hand) are **built in and locked**: they cannot be renamed or deleted, because the forecast keys off `Refund`, `Transfer` and `BNPL`. They live in code (`domain/categories.py`), not in the database. (2) Any other category is **the person's own**: added by hand (table `user_categories`, migration 0002, unique on `(user_id, lower(name))`), or typed in their own spreadsheet and imported as written (D-42), in which case it exists only on its transactions until they manage it. `GET /api/categories` lists built-in first, then their own (table names plus every other name found on transactions), each with a transaction count. (3) **Add** refuses a name that exists in any case, including a built-in. **Rename** moves every one of the caller's transactions to the new name, refuses a name that is taken (case-only changes of the same category are allowed), and keeps the new name in the table so it survives even if its transactions are later deleted. **Delete** of a category that has transactions requires `move_to`, which must be an existing, pickable category other than itself; moving into another of their own categories **merges** the two. (4) Names travel in a body or query string, never in the path ("Drink/Dessert" has a slash). (5) A name is normalised (NFKC, so a fullwidth look-alike of a built-in name is that name) and refused if it holds control, zero-width, bidirectional or other invisible characters; a built-in name typed by hand in any case (`food`, `TRANSFER`) is stored spelled the app's way, so it is never a look-alike that cannot be managed. (6) The transaction form, the filters and the import review take their choices from this list and fall back to the built-in set plus the categories already on the ledger if the list cannot be fetched.
**Why.** Her own file brought in "Boyfriend Tax", "Self Care" and "Self-Care", "Drink / Dessert": real categories the app could not manage, and a spelling split ("Self Care" / "Self-Care") that only a merge fixes. The category names do not change any forecast or risk number, except the three reserved ones, so the locked set is the minimum that must not be edited.
**Rejected.** Storing the built-in categories per user (a migration and a seed for no benefit; the engines key off the names); letting the built-in ones be renamed (the importer would keep guessing the old name); a path-based API (names with a slash); learn-from-corrections rules ("always label SAL CATERING as Food"): useful for noisy bank descriptions, recorded as future work; a free-text-only field (what existed).
**Trade-off.** Migration 0002 must be applied to the real Supabase project before the Categories page works there; until then the pages fall back and "Add transaction" and the import review keep working. Two categories that differ only in case can coexist if they arrived from files (the table is case-insensitive, transactions are not); the way to merge them is delete-with-move. Counting a person's categories reads their whole ledger (a grouped count in Postgres would be cheaper; deferred, with the rate limit as the guard). A rename is not one database transaction: if the move of transactions fails partway the name is put back, and if that also fails both names are listed and deleting the old one with `move_to` the new one finishes it.

## D-44 — Merchant text ignores month names

## D-47 — Competition variant: an assistant layer where the LLM narrates and proposes, the engines decide
*2026-10-17 · author decision (FinTechathon 2026, Topic A); overrides D-05 for the assistant layer only*

**Decision.** PaySense v3 adds an assistant (`backend/app/assistant/`) that turns requests ("can I afford an RM799 phone on SPayLater over 3 months?") into calls on the existing domain functions and engines, in process. The LLM (Claude via the Anthropic API, model from `ASSISTANT_MODEL`, key `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` server-side only) **narrates and proposes; it never produces a score, forecast or amount**. Every figure shown comes from a tool result rendered as a card; every figure in the narration is checked against the tool results and the user's message, and a template sentence replaces any narration that fails. With no key, or with the LLM unavailable, the same tools run in guided mode (buttons and forms, template wording). D-05's fixed candidate lists for both engines are unchanged.
**Why.** The competition theme is "an AI agent that completes real tasks"; v2 is decision support with no agent. Keeping the numbers in the engines preserves everything v2 measured and keeps the LLM out of the money path.
**Rejected.** The LLM computing affordability itself (unverifiable numbers, breaks D-05 and the no-fabricated-numbers rule); a scripted chatbot with no model (does not meet the theme); calling the LLM from the browser (exposes the key and widens the CSP).
**Trade-off.** A third-party processor sees derived figures (privacy notes and consent in WP19); cost and availability depend on the provider, bounded by per-user and global daily caps. `ASSUMED:` six tool calls per turn suffice for the hero workflow.

## D-48 — Three permission tiers; writes only through a user-confirmed pending action
*2026-10-17 · author decision; design by Claude*

**Decision.** Tier 0 tools read the signed-in user's data; Tier 1 tools compute or parse and change nothing (risk check, plan preview, budget suggestion, paste parsing, `propose_action`); Tier 2 functions write (add transaction, confirm import, create plan, set or delete a budget). **The model has no Tier 2 tool.** `propose_action` validates the payload with the API's own Pydantic models and stores a pending action (table `assistant_actions`: per user, expiring, single use). The UI renders the confirm card from the validated payload; only `POST /api/assistant/actions/{id}/confirm`, after the user presses Confirm, runs the Tier 2 function, after checking same user, not expired, still pending (atomic status change). `user_id` always comes from the session; any `user_id` in tool arguments is rejected. Every message, tool call, proposal, confirmation, refusal and fallback is written to `assistant_events`.
**Why.** This is the "permission-tier implementation" the security self-assessment is scored on, and it makes prompt injection unable to write: the worst an injected instruction can do is propose, and a person sees the exact payload.
**Rejected.** Giving the model write tools behind a "confirm" sentence in the chat (the model could skip it); a signed stateless token for the pending action (no single use, no audit row).
**Trade-off.** One extra click per write; a pending action expires (`ASSUMED:` 10 minutes) and must be proposed again.

## D-52 — Artefact settings digests ignore line endings
*2026-10-17 · Claude decision (found on the first Linux run)*

**Decision.** The Engine 2 artefact check accepts the forecast-settings file (`models/engine1/v1/metadata.json`) when its stored digest matches the file with LF or with CRLF line endings; new exports record the LF digest.
**Why.** The artefact was trained on a Windows checkout, where git writes CRLF, so it recorded the CRLF digest; a Linux checkout (and the Docker image for Render) holds LF, so the server refused to load the model and 17 tests failed. **Rejected.** Editing the stored digest by hand (an artefact is written by its script); forcing CRLF in `.gitattributes` (breaks the next Linux export the other way).
**Trade-off.** Any other change to the file still refuses the model, as before.

## D-54 — The forecast band is widened by history length and series (C2)
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision during WP16 (replaces D-35's single 1.5 factor)*

**Decision.** The user's error record is widened by a factor read from `backend/models/engine1/band_inflation.json`, chosen by weeks of history (from 17, 26, 39, 52) and series (spend, income): spend 2.8 / 2.4 / 2.1 / 2.2, income 1.1 / 1.3 / 1.7 / 1.2. The table is fitted by `ml.engine1_eval.band_calibration` on 300 development students of the Engine 1 cohort (indices 4,000–9,999, not multiples of ten): per bucket, the smallest factor on a 1.0–6.0 grid whose P10–P90 holds the real 13-week total 80% of the time, else the factor with the best coverage. It is checked on the 60 held-out Engine 1 test students. The Engine 2 artefact records the table's digest and refuses a different table.
**Why.** With one factor of 1.5 the band held the real total 61% of the time for spending and 50% for income on held-out students, so P(shortfall) was more extreme than the evidence supports. With the table: 69% and 53%; about 78–79% for spending from 26 weeks of history and 79% for income from 52 weeks.
**Rejected / Trade-off.** A single larger factor (over-widens long histories). A factor that never shrinks for shorter histories (planned; forcing it made held-out income coverage 0.50 → 0.21, because weekly draws are clipped at zero and the simulated income is off-centre, so a wider band can hold the total less often — ASSUMED 40). Widening cannot reach 80% for 17–25-week histories (0.38 spending, 0.33 income) or for income below 52 weeks; a population error record or an income level correction would be needed and is left to the author (`questions.md` row 22). Synthetic students only; no real-data check of the table (row 25). **Disclosed after the ml-audit (M2):** dropping the never-shrink rule was decided after seeing held-out coverage, so the 60 held-out Engine 1 test students are no longer an untouched check of the final table; judge the choice on the development rows of `reports/engine1/band_calibration.md`. A fresh check on 300 further students from the same pool (none of them development, lockbox or Engine 1 evaluation students; `reports/engine1/band_fresh_check.md`, `python -m ml.engine1_eval.band_fresh_check`) agrees with the held-out check: spending 0.72 overall (held-out 0.69), 0.79–0.81 from 26 weeks; income 0.53 (held-out 0.53).

## D-55 — Export parity accepts a row only when a float32 split flip is proven
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision during WP16 (amends D-40's 1e-3 parity gate)*

**Decision.** `ml.engine2_train.export` still requires ONNX and served predictions within 1e-3 of the trained model. A row beyond that passes only if walking the LightGBM trees shows a split that goes one way with the double threshold and the other with its float32 copy (which ONNX stores), and never beyond 1e-2. Each such row is recorded in `metadata.json` (`explained_by_float32_thresholds`), and the committed-model test enforces the same rule.
**Why.** The v2 export refused on one of 3,000 rows (0.00144): `limit_utilisation_with_purchase` = 0.9999924187 lies between LightGBM's threshold 0.9999924144 and its float32 copy 0.9999924302. That is the ONNX format, not a conversion bug.
**Rejected / Trade-off.** Raising the tolerance for every row (would hide a real conversion error). Snapping features to float32 at training (changes every label pairing for one boundary row). The served number can differ from the trained model by up to 0.01 on a row that sits exactly on a split.

## D-56 — Serving keeps the user's forecast bias; re-centring is a sensitivity analysis (C6)
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision during WP16*

**Decision.** Serving keeps centring the simulated paths on the user's own error record, including its mean (a level the model has been getting wrong is carried forward). `labels --recentre-errors` removes that mean for a second label run used only in `reports/engine2/versions.md`; nothing trains on it.
**Why.** Measured, not assumed: re-centring moves the label by −0.014 on average (mean absolute 0.043, 95th percentile 0.215) and keeps the same band for 91.8% of 10,000 students; irregular-income students move the other way (+0.035). The bias is small on average and has no consistent sign across personas, so removing it is not clearly better.
**Rejected / Trade-off.** Serving re-centred errors (would discard real evidence that the model is off for this user). The income median is about +35% of the recent level at the old factor (`band_calibration.md`) — widening does not move that, and neither does this decision.

## D-58 — An in-app security drill for the four named attack classes
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision after the competitive-edge research (author to confirm)*

**Decision.** A signed-in user can run a **security drill**: `POST /api/security/drill` builds a throwaway in-memory sandbox (two synthetic students, secret markers) and runs a fixed list of attacks — at least three for each of the four named classes (induced transfers, data extraction, privilege escalation, prompt injection) — through the real assistant code with a hostile scripted model that obeys every injected instruction. For each attack it reports what the model was made to try ("fooled") and what happened to the sandbox data ("harm"), measured on the sandbox afterwards. The in-memory database moves from `tests/` into `app/db/memory.py` so the server can build the sandbox.

**Why.** Security is 30% of the rubric and is judged against these four classes; rivals show scoreboards on screen; a pytest suite reads as self-reported. Assuming the model is always fooled shows that the defences do not depend on the model behaving.

**Rejected / Trade-off.** Running attacks against the caller's own data (a bug would harm a real account); a live model in the drill (tokens, nondeterminism — the live evaluation in `reports/assistant/` covers real-model behaviour); a static evidence page only (not reproducible on screen). Cost: test-grade in-memory database code now ships in the server image, used only by the drill.

## D-60 — Engine 1 floors each simulated month at zero, not each week (F1)
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision, author to confirm (engine v3 round; amends D-35 and D-54)*

**Decision.** `simulate.simulate` takes `clip = "week" | "month"`. With `"month"` each path's weekly draws of discretionary spending and of unconfirmed income are first split into calendar months by days (the same weights as before) and only each **month's** total is floored at zero; the point forecast is still floored per week, and the weekly feature summaries (`disc_weekly_p50/p90`) keep a per-week floor. The mode is a field of the band rule file (`clip`), so the digest Engine 2 already records covers it; a rule without the field is `"week"` (v2 behaviour).
**Why.** Flooring every simulated week deletes the negative part of the error draws and raises every simulated total: on the fresh group the served table's simulated median was +0.138 (spending) and +0.260 (income) of the recent level too high, and a wider band could hold the real total less often (ASSUMED 40). With the monthly floor (and D-61, refit) the bias is +0.006 and +0.012 (`reports/engine1/band_v3.md`).
**Rejected / Trade-off.** Flooring only the 13-week total (cannot apply to a running monthly margin); a level correction table (F5: a second fitted table, double correction). A single simulated week can now be below zero inside a month: a month cannot. Served only through D-64's rule.

## D-61 — Below 26 weeks of history the error record is a population record (F2)
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision, author to confirm (engine v3 round; answers questions.md 22)*

**Decision.** For a user with fewer than 26 complete weeks, each series' error record is a pooled set of 13-week error paths written as a share of the recent level ((actual − forecast) / mean of the latest 26 weeks, all of a shorter history), times the user's own recent level; from 26 weeks the user's own record is used, as before (not mixed). The record is fitted by `ml.engine1_eval.band_v3` on 300 new development students (group P: one seeded window per history length 17, 19, 21, 23, 25 weeks, spread across the year; 1,500 spending and 1,500 income paths, no window had a zero level; mean ratio error +0.22 for both, i.e. the LSTM under-forecasts short histories and the record carries that into the paths, as D-56 does for a user's own record), stored as `models/engine1/population_errors.json`, named with its SHA-256 in the band rule, refused by the loader on any other digest, and recorded by Engine 2 (`POPULATION_RECORD_SHA256`, D-40 style).
**Why.** Widening a short history's own record could not reach 80% (0.41 spending, 0.30 income at 17–25 weeks on the fresh group); a user with exactly 17 weeks had a single-path fallback record that the path bootstrap can only replay, i.e. a band of zero width. With the population record: 0.78 and 0.82.
**Rejected / Trade-off.** Mixing own and population paths by history length (another fitted weight); conformal intervals (no paths, D-04). Only the level is the user's below 26 weeks: personalisation of the spread is lost. Synthetic students only, and the cohort holds total income while serving forecasts residual income (note N2, F3 not done).

## D-62 — Engine 2 learns the purchase's added share: a two-part target (G1)
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision, author to confirm (engine v3 round; amends D-07's framing, D-10, D-12 and D-40)*

**Decision.** The label stays Engine 1's simulated P(short with the plan) (D-07), written p_with = b + (1 − b)·q, with b the simulated chance without the purchase (shared seed) and q the share of the not-short futures the purchase makes short. Every candidate (D-05, same monotone directions) learns q from the 17 features with weight (1 − b) on top of duplicate-and-weight; every CV, tuning and lockbox score is on the composed b + (1 − b)·q̂ against the label; the calibrator is chosen on that composed Brier and fitted on q̂. Served, b is the request's own simulation (already computed) and the shown probability is b + (1 − b)·q̂; the artefact says `target: two_part` and the loader refuses an unknown target. Reasons are the exact baseline Shapley values of q̂ (same reference student) times (1 − b), so they are on the shown scale and are about the purchase; the 0.02 threshold is unchanged. The API contract does not change.
**Why.** 71% of the label's variance was the starting position (R4): the v2 model mostly reproduced b. On the new lockbox the two-part LightGBM has Brier 0.0078 against 0.0532 for predicting b alone, skill 0.951 (v2 0.915 on its own lockbox); the serving check puts the shown number in the simulation's band 95.5% of the time (v2 92.0%).
**Rejected / Trade-off.** b as a feature (G2: the reasons would mostly say "your starting risk"). The shown number carries the Monte Carlo noise of b (1,000 paths). **Unflattering:** with reasons about the purchase, 56.6% of non-Safe results on the serving check show "nothing stands out" (v2: 0%), because for a student already short the purchase adds little; the screen's without / with / added block (D-57) is what explains those results. Where the purchase moves the band (116 lockbox profiles) the model's band agreement is 76.7%, the same as the constant's and above the logistic's 69.8%.

## D-63 — The savings feature is measured in months of expected outgoings (G5)
*2026-10-18 · Claude decision, author to confirm (engine v3 round; amends D-50's feature; answers questions.md 34)*
