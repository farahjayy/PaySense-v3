## Q1. Malaysia: what does the Consumer Credit Act 2025 require of BNPL providers, who regulates, and when?

### Takeaway
The Consumer Credit Act 2025 (Act 873) is **IN FORCE** (from 1 March 2026) and puts non-bank BNPL under a new regulator, the Consumer Credit Commission (Suruhanjaya Kredit Pengguna, SKP). Licensing applications opened 1 June 2026, with a roughly six-month transition for existing providers. The conduct duties are a creditworthiness and affordability check before credit is granted, transparent and reasonable fees, rules for ads and missed payments, ethical debt collection, complaints handling and hardship assistance. No cooling-off right was found in any source.

### Cited Findings
**Status, regulator and dates**
- The Act was gazetted on 31 December 2025 and came into force on 1 March 2026. The government set up the Suruhanjaya Kredit Pengguna (Consumer Credit Commission, SKP) — [SKP media release](https://www.skp.gov.my/en/news/media-releases/enforcement-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025-and-the-establishment-of-the-consumer-credit-commission); [DFDL summary](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/content-hub/articles/malaysia-a-summary-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025/)
- The licensing rules took effect on 1 June 2026, and BNPL firms have six months from that date to comply — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/794624). SKP's FAQ says licensing and registration applications opened on 1 June 2026 — [SKP FAQs](https://www.skp.gov.my/en/reference/faqs)
- **Conflict on when the transition ends:** secondary sources say around 30 November 2026 ([malaysia4u guide](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)), but the DFDL summary says 1 June 2026 to 31 December 2026 ([DFDL](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/content-hub/articles/malaysia-a-summary-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025/)). The SKP primary text should settle it.
- BNPL providers, leasing firms and factoring companies must hold a licence from the Commission, which regulates non-bank credit and credit service providers — [Lowyat.NET](https://www.lowyat.net/2025/360250/dewan-rakyat-passes-consumer-credit-bill-bnpl/); [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/794624)
- Earlier coverage named a "Consumer Credit Oversight Board (CCOB)" as the regulator. That name is now out of date and the regulator is SKP — [Bernama (CCOB era)](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2493367); [malaysia4u guide](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)
- The regulatory framework was developed jointly by MOF, BNM and the Securities Commission — [MOF press citation](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-citations/mof-bnm-sc-to-formulate-framework-on-consumer-credit-regulation). Note: bank-run BNPL is still governed by BNM (see Q2), not by SKP.

**Creditworthiness and affordability**
- Deputy Finance Minister Liew Chin Tong said every BNPL company must carry out creditworthiness and affordability assessments before approving any financing — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/790792); [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnpl-providers-required-to-run-credit-checks-under-consumer-credit-act-2025.html). Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah Azizan made the same point earlier — [Lowyat.NET](https://www.lowyat.net/2025/361524/bnpl-providers-must-conduct-affordability-assessments-under-consumer-credit-act/)
- The assessment starts at onboarding. At a minimum it must consider the consumer's repayment history and insolvency status. Providers must not lend to someone who is insolvent, likely to default, or expected to struggle to repay in full — law-firm summaries in [HHQ](https://hhq.com.my/posts/how-to-conduct-a-bnpl-business-in-malaysia-under-the-cca-2025-top-10-key-takeaways/) and [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/malaysia-consumer-credit-act-2025/) (secondary; check against the SKP Conduct Standards)
- In its October 2026 parliamentary reply, the Finance Ministry said providers must check that a customer can afford to repay by looking at **total commitments, including bank and non-bank credit** — reported by [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767) and [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/14/malaysian-youths-rack-up-rm54bil-in-buy-now-pay-later-purchases). The Rakyat Post (16 Oct 2026) also describes "mandatory affordability checks before approving any purchases" — [The Rakyat Post](https://www.therakyatpost.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/16/malaysian-youths-make-4-in-10-bnpl-transactions-owe-rm2-4-billion/)

**Disclosure, advertising, fees and missed payments (Conduct Standards, per HHQ)**
- Ads must not suggest a service is free of interest, fees or qualifications without clearly stating the conditions or eligibility criteria, and disclaimers must not be hidden by the ad's content or design — [HHQ top-10 takeaways](https://hhq.com.my/posts/how-to-conduct-a-bnpl-business-in-malaysia-under-the-cca-2025-top-10-key-takeaways/)
- Terms, charges and fees must be transparent and reasonable and must not place an undue burden on users. Terms must be in plain language, and misleading ads or unfair late charges now carry legal penalties — [RinggitPlus explainer](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/the-experts-corner/malaysias-new-consumer-credit-act-explained.html); [Paydibs](https://paydibs.com/bnpl-new-regulation-2025-malaysia/)
- Missed payments: after a reminder, and once a consumer has missed up to two consecutive payments, the account is suspended until the missed payments and any late charges are paid. A provider may suspend after a single missed payment — HHQ summary of the Conduct Standards, as relayed in search extracts ([HHQ](https://hhq.com.my/posts/how-to-conduct-a-bnpl-business-in-malaysia-under-the-cca-2025-top-10-key-takeaways/)). Not verified against the primary text.
- Ethical debt collection, complaint-handling mechanisms and consideration of hardship assistance are required. The Commission can investigate, enforce and impose penalties — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/the-experts-corner/malaysias-new-consumer-credit-act-explained.html); [RAM Ratings](https://www.ram.com.my/pressrelease/?prviewid=7068)
- Merchant angle: the Act changes merchant liability and onboarding for BNPL checkouts, and providers must run more rigorous affordability assessments — [Curlec](https://curlec.com/blog/bnpl-2-0-what-the-consumer-credit-act-2025-means-for-merchant-liability/)

### Inferences
- Under the CCA, Malaysia's affordability rule is principles-based ("can afford to repay", "total commitments", "repayment history", "insolvency"). No search result showed a statutory RM threshold or debt-service-ratio figure for non-bank BNPL. The one hard number in Malaysian BNPL rules is BNM's RM1,500 cumulative-limit trigger for bank-run BNPL (Q2).
- "Total commitments, including bank and non-bank credit" is the Malaysian version of the anti-stacking principle. It is also exactly the cross-provider view a consumer app can build from the user's own ledger.
- A student app is not a credit provider, so none of these duties bind PaySense. They can be mirrored as consumer-side checks, framed as "the check your lender is supposed to run, done on your own numbers".

### Gaps
- **No source found on a cooling-off or withdrawal right** for BNPL under the CCA 2025. Treat it as absent or unknown, not as present.
- **No source found on a statutory late-fee cap** (ringgit amount or percentage) under the CCA or SKP Conduct Standards. Only "reasonable / no undue burden" language was found.
- **No age restriction (under-21, students) or income-based limit** found for Malaysian BNPL. A claimed "RM3,000 income / RM1,000 limit for young users" rule could not be sourced. The nearest real rule is Indonesia's OJK requirement (from 2027) that BNPL users be at least 18 with a minimum monthly income of IDR 3 million ([AsiaNews](https://www.asianews.it/en/south-east-asia/indonesia/govt-imposes-new-rules-for-buy-now-pay-later-as-debt-rises-among-young-people)), which is probably the source of that confusion.
- The SKP Conduct Standards / guidelines document itself was not retrieved. The suspension-after-missed-payments detail and the minimum creditworthiness inputs rest on HHQ's summary.
- Licence status of specific providers (Shopee SPayLater, Atome, Grab PayLater) under the new regime was not found.

## Q2. Malaysia: BNM's rules for bank-run BNPL (Policy Document on Personal Financing, 30 Sept 2025)

### Takeaway
BNM's replacement Policy Document on Personal Financing (BNM/RH/PD 028-130, issued 30 September 2025) is **IN FORCE** on issuance, but some paragraphs only take effect from 1 January 2027. For bank-run BNPL it requires a full affordability assessment once a consumer's **cumulative BNPL credit limit across all providers reaches RM1,500** (banks may set a lower trigger). The assessment means verifying income, establishing overall indebtedness and applying a prudent debt service ratio. Banks must also report BNPL to CCRIS as a facility named "Buy Now Pay Later".

### Cited Findings
- The PD was issued on 30 September 2025 as BNM/RH/PD 028-130 and replaced the December 2023 version — [BNM PD PDF](https://www.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124/938039/pd-pf-sep25.pdf); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6a4b8a2f-08cf-4a44-8d81-08df2995153c)
- **RM1,500 cumulative trigger:** a financial service provider (FSP) must run an affordability assessment for a consumer whose cumulative BNPL credit limit is RM1,500 or more. "Cumulative" means the total BNPL limits granted to that consumer by **all BNPL providers, including the FSP**. An FSP may set a lower threshold — [Skrine alert](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on); [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/41291/fintech-lending-malaysia/bnms-new-personal-financing-policy-defines-fis-approach-to-bnpl-offerings/)
- The assessment must establish whether the consumer can repay the new BNPL facility in full while keeping a **prudent debt service ratio (DSR)** as set out in BNM's Responsible Financing policy document. The FSP must obtain and verify income and establish overall indebtedness from existing debt repayments — [Skrine](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cc80cb96-54f3-403f-8f9c-3801131bcb20)
- Below the threshold, lenders must still assess repayment capacity, using credit history or utility bill payments — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2025/09/30/bnm-tightens-rule-on-personal-financing-and-bnpl-bans-flat-rate-loans/)
- **CCRIS:** an FSP must report both conventional and Islamic BNPL to the Central Credit Reference Information System (CCRIS) as a facility named "Buy Now Pay Later", in a standardised format — [Skrine](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on); [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/54404/regtech-fintech-regulation-malaysia/bnm-just-tightened-malaysias-personal-financing-policy-heres-what-changed/)
- The same PD bans flat-rate personal financing — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2025/09/30/bnm-tightens-rule-on-personal-financing-and-bnpl-bans-flat-rate-loans/)
- **Phasing:** the PD took effect on issuance, but paragraphs 10.3–10.13, 10.17 and 10.18 take effect on 1 January 2027 — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cc80cb96-54f3-403f-8f9c-3801131bcb20). Fintech News reports that reporting and disclosure requirements apply immediately, while detailed disclosure rules and financial-education modules apply from 1 January 2027 — [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/54404/regtech-fintech-regulation-malaysia/bnm-just-tightened-malaysias-personal-financing-policy-heres-what-changed/)

### Inferences
- RM1,500 cumulative across all providers is the most concrete, citable and demoable affordability threshold in Malaysian BNPL rules. An app can show a running cross-provider total of BNPL limits or commitments against an RM1,500 line, labelled as the point where BNM requires a bank lender to run a full affordability check.
- BNM's DSR concept (repayments as a share of income) maps directly onto a student's monthly obligations versus income/allowance, which the PaySense ledger already holds. No DSR percentage for students was found, so the app should not invent one (CLAUDE.md hard constraint 5). It can show the user's own ratio and explain the concept.

### Gaps
- Whether the RM1,500 rule (reported as para 17) and the CCRIS rule fall in the immediately effective group or the 1 January 2027 group could not be confirmed without the PDF.
- The prudent DSR figure itself (in the separate Responsible Financing PD) was not retrieved.
- Whether BNM's PD sets any rule specific to young or low-income consumers was not found.

## Q3. Malaysia: is BNPL reported to CCRIS or private credit bureaus (CTOS, Experian, RAMCI) in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
**Bank-run BNPL: yes.** The BNM PD requires CCRIS reporting as a "Buy Now Pay Later" facility. **Non-bank BNPL (SPayLater, Atome, Grab, etc.): sources conflict.** Officials and mainstream press say BNPL data must reach CCRIS or credit reporting agencies such as CTOS. A consumer guide says the CCA has no credit-reporting duty, and policy commentary treats non-bank data visibility as still a gap.

### Cited Findings
- BNM PD: FSPs report conventional and Islamic BNPL to CCRIS as "Buy Now Pay Later" — [Skrine](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on)
- Search extracts of The Edge / Lowyat coverage of the deputy minister's statement: BNPL providers must report consumers' credit data to credit reporting agencies such as CTOS via a centralised database — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/790792); [Lowyat.NET](https://www.lowyat.net/2025/361524/bnpl-providers-must-conduct-affordability-assessments-under-consumer-credit-act/)
- The Star says the Consumer Credit Act requires BNPL facilities to be reflected in CCRIS — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/05/27/rising-credit-commitments-highlight-need-for-stronger-financial-awareness-among-middle-income-earner)
- **Contradicted by:** a consumer guide saying the CCA has no credit-reporting provisions, that non-bank BNPL generally does not appear on CCRIS or CTOS, and that the viral claim of BNM-mandated CCRIS reporting from May 2024 is unsupported — [malaysia4u guide](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)
- RinggitPlus notes that CCRIS remains the main source of banking-related credit information, and that credit reporting agencies can widen visibility into borrowing outside the banking system. This reads as a policy aim, not a settled requirement — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnpl-use-among-malaysian-youth-raises-concern-as-new-consumer-credit-law-takes-effect.html)

### Inferences
- The safest wording for an app is: "Bank-offered BNPL is reported to CCRIS. Many non-bank BNPL providers share data with private credit bureaus such as CTOS, and late payments may affect future credit applications. Check your CCRIS/CTOS report." That avoids asserting the unresolved statutory point.
- Whatever the legal answer, lenders cannot see across providers today. That is the argument for a user-held cross-provider exposure view.

### Gaps
- The primary CCA text or SKP guideline on credit reporting was not retrieved, and the conflict could not be resolved.
- No CTOS / Experian Malaysia / RAMCI public statement on which BNPL providers report to them was found.

## Q4. Malaysia: AKPK statements, youth BNPL statistics and age restrictions

### Takeaway
Official Finance Ministry figures show young Malaysians are heavy BNPL users. Users aged 30 and below made 37.9% of transactions (RM5.4b) in H1 2026, with RM2.4b outstanding and 4.0% non-performing, which is higher than the national overdue ratio. AKPK warns against using BNPL as a long-term solution and promotes a "3P" test (Purpose, Payment capacity, Payment history). No age or student-specific restriction exists in Malaysia.

### Cited Findings
**Market statistics (Finance Ministry figures, via press)**
- H1 2025: 102.6 million BNPL transactions worth RM9.3b, up from RM7.1b in H2 2024. Outstanding was RM3.8b at 30 June 2025 (about 0.2% of household debt), and overdue was RM121.8m (3.2%). There were 6.5 million active accounts, and 168,967 (2.6%) were overdue — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnpl-booms-in-malaysia-with-rm9-3-billion-in-spending-what-you-should-know.html); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/2025/08/04/malaysias-buy-now-pay-later-loans-hit-rm38b-but-risks-under-control-ahead-of-new-rules-says-amir-hamzah/186427). Malay Mail's "3.1%" growth figure looks like a typo for 31%.
- H1 2025: about 40% of transactions were by users aged 30 and below — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2025/08/07/bnpl-transactions-in-malaysia-surge-to-over-rm9-billion-in-six-months-youth-debt-a-concern/); [The Vibes](https://www.thevibes.com/articles/news/111314/bnpl-transactions-surge-past-rm9-billion-in-h1-2025-with-40-attributed-to-youths)
- Q1 2026: outstanding was RM5.3b, overdue 3.4% (RM181m), across about 8 million account holders — [The Vibes](https://www.thevibes.com/index.php/articles/news/125094/bnpl-users-hit-eight-million-as-outstanding-balances-reach-rm5.3b) (the malaysia4u guide gives the same figures)
- H1 2026, from a Finance Ministry written parliamentary reply (13–14 Oct 2026): users aged 30 and below made 65.9 million BNPL transactions worth RM5.4b. That is 37.9% of the 173.9 million national transactions and 36.7% of the RM14.7b national value. At 30 June 2026 their outstanding balance was RM2.4b, of which RM96.8m (4.0%) was non-performing — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/14/malaysian-youths-rack-up-rm54bil-in-buy-now-pay-later-purchases); [The Sun](https://thesun.my/news/young-malaysians-spend-rm5-4-billion-through-bnpl-schemes-in-six-months/); [Media Selangor](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/10/401038); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3370811/young-malaysians-us13bn-short-term-credit-spree-sparks-warning-over-bad-debt)
- Deputy Finance Minister Lim Hui Ying raised concern about risks to low-income groups with limited financial literacy — [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/07/21/finance-ministry-bnpl-transactions-hit-rm93b-in-first-half-of-2025-as-low-income-malaysians-face-rising-debt-risks/184678)

**AKPK**
- In March 2025, AKPK warned that BNPL is not a long-term financial solution and urged consumers to ask "do you really need that right now?" — [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/03/25/do-you-really-need-that-right-now-bnpl-can-be-useful-but-not-long-term-financial-solution-akpk-warns/170730)
- AKPK's "3P" formula for responsible short-term credit, including BNPL, is Purpose, Payment Capacity and Payment History — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/748191)
- AKPK (April 2024): about 53,000 youths aged 30 and below were in debt totalling nearly RM1.9b (credit cards and loans) — [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/04/23/akpk-over-50000-youths-in-debt-due-to-credit-cards-loans-worth-close-to-rm2b-in-total/130302)
- Political calls to give AKPK a bigger role in youth financial education — [FMT](https://fmtv5.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2025/01/23/empower-akpk-to-educate-youth-on-financial-management-says-dap-man)

### Inferences
- Young users' 4.0% non-performing ratio (H1 2026) is above the national overdue ratios of 3.2% (mid-2025) and 3.4% (Q1 2026). The definitions differ ("non-performing" versus "overdue"), so present this as indicative, not like-for-like.
- AKPK's 3P maps one-to-one onto a pre-purchase checklist. Purpose is a user-answered "need now?" prompt, Payment capacity is the Engine 1 shortfall forecast, and Payment history is the user's own on-time record in the ledger. AKPK is the natural hardship referral for Malaysian users.

### Gaps
- No AKPK statistic specific to BNPL (for example, the share of AKPK DMP enrolees holding BNPL debt) was found for 2025–2026.
- No Malaysian minimum age, student ban or youth limit for BNPL was found in the CCA, BNM PD or press. Malaysia's standard age of majority is 18, but that was not verified in this session as a BNPL eligibility rule.

## Q5. UK: FCA regulation of Deferred Payment Credit (DPC)

### Takeaway
**IN FORCE from 15 July 2026.** The FCA's final rules are PS26/1, published 11 February 2026. They apply the existing CONC 5.2A creditworthiness rules to third-party interest-free BNPL, including loans under £50, with proportionate assessments. They also cover pre-contract disclosure, information when a payment is missed, Consumer Duty, Financial Ombudsman Service access and withdrawal rights. Merchant-provided "own" DPC is outside scope.

### Cited Findings
- PS26/1 was published on 11 February 2026. DPC means interest-free credit for goods or services, repayable in 12 or fewer instalments within 12 months. The rules apply from 15 July 2026 — [FCA PS26/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps26-1-regulation-deferred-payment-credit); [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/uk-fca-finalises-bnpl-rules-deferred-payment-credit); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/fca-confirms-final-rules-for-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-sector-or-deferred-payment-credit-ahead-of-new-regime/)
- Third-party DPC lenders need FCA authorisation or a temporary permission. The temporary permissions notification window ran from 15 May to 1 July 2026. Merchant-provided own DPC and DPC broking are generally exempt — [TLT](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/getting-ready-for-the-fcas-buy-now-pay-later-regime); [PKF](https://www.pkf-l.com/insights/fca-bnpl-regulation-2026-rules-timeline-compliance/); [FCA BNPL page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/regulating-buy-now-pay-later)
- **Affordability:** existing CONC 5.2A creditworthiness rules apply to DPC, including agreements under £50. Firms should use judgement so that each assessment is proportionate — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/fca-confirms-final-rules-for-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-sector-or-deferred-payment-credit-ahead-of-new-regime/); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-fintech-and-digital-assets/buy-now-pay-later-the-final-furlong-policy-statement-ps26-1-regulation-of-deferred-payment-credit)
- **Credit reference agencies (CRAs) in disclosure:** a firm must say whether it will (where known), or may, obtain information from a CRA, because lenders may not know when they provide key product information whether a CRA check will be needed — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-fintech-and-digital-assets/buy-now-pay-later-the-final-furlong-policy-statement-ps26-1-regulation-of-deferred-payment-credit)
- **Disclosure lifecycle:** lenders must give pre-contractual information, information during the agreement, and information when a customer misses a payment. Clear upfront information covers payment dates, amounts and the consequences of a missed payment — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/fca-confirms-final-rules-for-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-sector-or-deferred-payment-credit-ahead-of-new-regime/); [HAT Group](https://hatgroup.co.uk/fca-regulation-of-deferred-payment-credit-from-15-july-2026/)
- **Missed payments:** lenders must contact customers promptly when a payment is missed and explain the amount outstanding, any fees and the possible consequences — [SEON](https://seon.io/resources/buy-now-pay-later-uk-regulation-what-changed/); [CCTA](https://www.ccta.co.uk/getting-ready-bnpl-regulation/)
- **Withdrawal:** the consultation (CP25/23) said consumers would have withdrawal rights under section 66A of the Consumer Credit Act 1974. In the final rules, information about withdrawal or cancellation rights, early repayment and the right to complain to the Financial Ombudsman moved from the key information into an "additional product information" document given before the agreement — [FCA CP25/23](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp25-23.pdf); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/uk-bnpl-regime-fca-publishes-final-rules-102mkqc)
- **Wider framework:** CONC 5.2A, DISP, section 75, Consumer Duty and the Ombudsman's compulsory jurisdiction apply to regulated DPC agreements taken out from 15 July 2026 — [Claims Bible commentary](https://claimsbible.com/unaffordable-lending/buy-now-pay-later-affordability/) (secondary; one source only for section 75)
- **Credit reporting:** the FCA proposed requiring BNPL lenders to share agreement data with designated CRAs so that BNPL appears on credit files and lenders can see over-borrowing. Some firms already report: Zilch to all major UK CRAs, and Klarna to Experian and TransUnion — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/insights/bnpl-regulation-back-in-focus/); [House of Commons Library briefing (29 July 2026)](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10328/CBP-10328.pdf)

### Inferences
- The FCA's three disclosure moments (before the contract, during the agreement, on a missed payment) map neatly onto three app screens: a pre-purchase "key facts" card, an instalment timeline, and a missed-payment consequence explainer.
- Section 66A of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 is the statutory 14-day withdrawal right for regulated credit. The 14-day length is general knowledge of s66A and was not confirmed for DPC by a source in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- The PS26/1 text was not opened, so the exact final position on the 14-day withdrawal period for DPC and on whether CRA reporting is mandatory (rather than proposed) is unconfirmed.
- No UK statistic on multi-provider BNPL stacking was found.

## Q6. US: CFPB BNPL research and the withdrawn interpretive rule

### Takeaway
The US has no BNPL-specific affordability rule. The CFPB's 2024 interpretive rule treating Pay-in-4 lenders as credit card issuers under Regulation Z was **WITHDRAWN** on 12 May 2025. The CFPB's research is still the best evidence base on stacking and financial strain: 63% of borrowers held simultaneous BNPL loans and 33% did so across multiple firms (2022 data, published January 2025). BNPL users are also more likely to overdraft or use payday loans (2023 Making Ends Meet report).

### Cited Findings
- **2022 market report:** the report identified two overextension risks, **loan stacking** (multiple BNPL loans from different providers at the same time) and **sustained usage** — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2022/09/cfpb-releases-long-awaited-report-on-buy-now-pay-later-industry); [Atlanta Fed](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/take-on-payments/2022/11/07/more-highlights-from-cfpb-bnpl-report); [Director Chopra's remarks](https://consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/director-chopras-prepared-remarks-on-the-release-of-the-cfpbs-buy-now-pay-later-report/)
- **March 2023 Making Ends Meet report:** BNPL borrowers were more likely to be highly indebted, revolve credit card balances or be delinquent, and more likely to use payday loans, pawn loans and **bank overdrafts**. Many of these differences pre-date BNPL use. The data came from a survey of 2,036 consumers linked to credit records, with self-reported BNPL use — [CFPB report](https://consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/consumer-use-of-buy-now-pay-later-insights-from-the-cfpb-making-ends-meet-survey); [CFPB press release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-publishes-new-findings-on-financial-profiles-of-buy-now-pay-later-borrowers/); [American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/payments/news/buy-now-pay-later-users-prone-to-bank-overdrafts-payday-loans-cfpb)
- **January 2025 stacking study (2022 data, six large firms matched to credit records):** 63% of borrowers originated multiple simultaneous BNPL loans at some point in the year, and 33% did so across multiple firms. BNPL loans defaulted at about 2%, compared with about 10% for credit cards — [CFPB press release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-research-reveals-heavy-buy-now-pay-later-use-among-borrowers-with-high-credit-balances-and-multiple-pay-in-four-loans/); [Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2025/01/cfpb-releases-report-highlighting-consumer-use-of-buy-now-pay-later-products/). Industry (American Fintech Council) disputed the framing — [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-bnpl-buy-now-pay-later-research/750651/)
- **Interpretive rule:** issued May 2024 (Pay-in-4 lenders treated as card issuers under Regulation Z: dispute rights, refunds, periodic statements) — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/06/cfpb-applies-credit-card-rules). In March 2025 the CFPB said it would not keep the rule — [CFS Review](https://www.cfsreview.com/2025/03/cfpb-indicates-that-it-will-rescind-buy-now-pay-later-interpretative-rule/); [Kilpatrick](https://ktslaw.com/blog/consumer-financial-services/2025/4/cfpb-shifts-course-on-buy-now). It was withdrawn on 12 May 2025 with other guidance — [CFPB compliance page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/consumer-cards-resources/buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-products/); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-05-15-cfpb-withdraws-guidance-documents-in-shift-from-nonbinding-policymaking)
- A Congressional Research Service report sets out the policy options, including a bill to apply parts of TILA to Pay-in-4 and widen CFPB supervision — [CRS R48858](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48858)

### Inferences
- "One in three borrowers stacked loans across firms" (CFPB, 2022 data) is the cleanest regulator-sourced statistic to justify a cross-provider exposure feature. It is US data and should be labelled as such in a Malaysian pitch.
- The overdraft and payday correlation supports PaySense's approach of reading BNPL risk off the user's whole cash-flow picture (Engine 1), not just the BNPL ledger.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 CFPB BNPL rule-making was found; the US federal position after the withdrawal is "no BNPL-specific rule". State-level BNPL laws (for example New York's BNPL licensing law) were not researched in this pass.

## Q7. Australia: BNPL as low-cost credit (from 10 June 2025)

### Takeaway
**IN FORCE since 10 June 2025.** BNPL providers need an Australian Credit Licence and AFCA membership, and must follow responsible lending obligations. They may opt into a "modified" regime that still requires an **unsuitability assessment**, with a rebuttable presumption that limits of **A$2,000 or less** meet the consumer's requirements and objectives. ASIC's RG 281 (May 2025) lists financial-vulnerability indicators, including repeated late fees across credit products.

### Cited Findings
- The low-cost credit contract (LCCC) regime commenced on 10 June 2025. Most consumer BNPL products are LCCCs if they meet the fee caps — [ASIC RG 281 (May 2025)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/2sgcg0y5/rg281-published-8-may-2025.pdf); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/a6cfa71f/bnpl-compliance-countdown)
- Providers can elect the modified responsible lending obligations or the standard ones. Even under the modified regime the steps at RG 281.23, including the unsuitability assessment, still apply — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2d3b7578-4dea-4a02-8ab1-b4e74d1930b0); [JWS](https://jws.com.au/what-we-think/asic-issues-guidance-to-the-buy-now-pay-later-sector/)
- **A$2,000 presumption:** there is a rebuttable presumption that LCCCs with a limit of A$2,000 or less meet the consumer's requirements and objectives. It covers only that limb, not the duty to make inquiries. Providers may assess a higher limit upfront to cover later increases for up to two years if conditions are met — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2d3b7578-4dea-4a02-8ab1-b4e74d1930b0); [Broker Daily](https://www.brokerdaily.au/regulation/20357-asic-reveals-bnpl-reform-modifications)
- **Vulnerability indicators** (ASIC): reliance on Centrelink payments, **repeated late-payment fees across credit products**, a credit history showing substantial debt, and signs of family violence or financial abuse — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2d3b7578-4dea-4a02-8ab1-b4e74d1930b0)
- Consumer groups warned that relying only on self-reported income and expenses may not be enough for an unsuitability assessment — [Joint consumer submission to ASIC](https://earg.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Joint-Submission-to-ASIC-on-draft-regulatory-guide-for-BNPL-products.pdf)
- Licence, AFCA membership, responsible lending and National Credit Code hardship provisions apply. LCCC providers get access to the credit reporting system as licensed credit providers — [Stripe explainer](https://stripe.com/en-ca/resources/more/bnpl-regulations-in-australia-what-businesses-need-to-know); [Law Council of Australia submission](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-05/c2024-504798-law-council-of-australia.pdf)
- **Fee caps (sources conflict, probably draft versus final):** total fees excluding default fees capped at A$200 in year one and A$125 in later years, with default fees around A$10 a month or A$120 a year; another report gives A$320 in year one and A$245 later — [Banking Day](https://www.bankingday.com/bnpl-fees-capped-at-320); [AFSL House](https://afslhouse.com.au/insights/bnpl-regulation-australia-licensing-compliance/)
- The AFIA BNPL Code is being folded into the AFIA Finance Industry Code of Practice, with the move expected in the first half of 2026 — [AFIA](https://afia.asn.au/AFIA-Buy-Now-Pay-Later-Code-of-Practice)

### Inferences
- Australia's "unsuitability" framing (the lender must show the credit is *not unsuitable*) suits a student app well: "Is this purchase *unsuitable* for you right now?" answered with the user's own forecast.
- ASIC's vulnerability indicators are concrete signals that can be computed from a user ledger: repeated late fees across providers, and a high share of income committed to debt.

### Gaps
- Final fee-cap figures in the Low Cost Credit Regulations were not confirmed.
- The RG 281 PDF was not opened, so specific inquiry and verification expectations for small limits were not extracted.

## Q8. EU: Consumer Credit Directive (EU) 2023/2225 ("CCD2")

### Takeaway
**ENACTED, applying from 20 November 2026.** CCD2 brings most BNPL into consumer credit law by removing the old exemption for interest-free credit repayable within three months. It requires a documented, proportionate creditworthiness assessment even for small BNPL loans, and gives consumers a right to be told immediately and free of charge the result of a database check used in that assessment. Member-state transposition is under way (for example, German draft bills in 2025).

### Cited Findings
- The directive applies from 20 November 2026 — [EUR-Lex CCD2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj/eng); [Banking.Vision](https://banking.vision/en/consumer-credit-directive-2026)
- BNPL is in scope. The old exemption for interest-free credit repayable within three months is removed, replaced by a narrow exemption for suppliers offering deferred payment directly (no third-party credit, short payment window) — [Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-second-consumer-credit-directive-scope-and-impact-for-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-providers); [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32023L2225)
- Creditworthiness must be assessed thoroughly, on sufficient and reliable information, and documented. It must be proportionate but substantial even for microloans and BNPL, and there are explainability expectations for automated decisions — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d9e1b6e9-382e-4730-bd74-b1b85ad34296); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/what-does-the-consumer-credit-directive-require/)
- Consumers have the right to be told immediately and free of charge the result of a database consultation used in the creditworthiness assessment (Art. 19(6), per search extract) — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj/eng)
- German implementation: draft bill published June 2025, government draft September 2025 — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/germany/buy-now-pay-later-bmjv-verffentlicht-gesetzentwurf-zur-umsetzung-der-neuen-verbraucherkreditrichtlin); [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/proposed-bill-implementing-directive-eu-2023-2225-on-credit-agreements-for-consumers-adopted); [ShippyPro](https://www.shippypro.com/blog/en/buy-now-pay-later-eu-consumer-credit-directive-ccd2)

### Inferences
- CCD2's emphasis on explaining automated credit decisions and telling consumers what a database check found supports PaySense's existing SHAP explanations. "Why the model rates this purchase risky" can be presented as mirroring the explainability direction of EU law.

### Gaps
- Exact CCD2 article numbers for the creditworthiness assessment, the withdrawal right (CCD regimes have a 14-day withdrawal right, but that was not confirmed from a source this session) and unsolicited-credit rules were not extracted.
- Transposition status by member state at October 2026 was not checked.

## Q9. What do these regimes say about loan stacking and the lack of visibility across providers?

### Takeaway
Every regime names the same failure: a BNPL lender cannot see a borrower's BNPL debt with other providers. The responses are cumulative-limit triggers (BNM: RM1,500 across all providers), "total commitments" tests (Malaysian MOF), mandatory or encouraged credit-file reporting (BNM CCRIS, UK CRAs, Australian credit reporting access) and documented assessments (EU). Evidence of stacking is strongest in the US (33% stacked across firms). None of these regimes gives the consumer a cross-provider view, and that gap is one a consumer app can fill.

### Cited Findings
- US: the CFPB identified loan stacking as a core overextension risk (2022) and measured 63% simultaneous and 33% cross-firm stacking (2022 data, published 2025) — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2022/09/cfpb-releases-long-awaited-report-on-buy-now-pay-later-industry); [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-research-reveals-heavy-buy-now-pay-later-use-among-borrowers-with-high-credit-balances-and-multiple-pay-in-four-loans/)
- Malaysia (bank BNPL): the affordability trigger counts limits from **all BNPL providers, including the FSP** — [Skrine](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on)
- Malaysia (CCA): affordability must consider total commitments, including bank and non-bank credit — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767)
- Malaysia: CCRIS covers banking credit, and wider visibility depends on credit reporting agencies — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnpl-use-among-malaysian-youth-raises-concern-as-new-consumer-credit-law-takes-effect.html)
- UK: CRA data-sharing is intended to give lenders a fuller view and stop consumers taking on BNPL beyond their means. Better visibility of loans from other providers is expected to mean some borrowers fail affordability — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/insights/bnpl-regulation-back-in-focus/)
- Australia: one ASIC vulnerability indicator is repeated late fees **across credit products**, which is itself a cross-provider signal — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2d3b7578-4dea-4a02-8ab1-b4e74d1930b0)

### Inferences
- Even with full credit reporting, lenders see bureau data with a lag and only at the moment of application. A student sees their own commitments across Shopee, Atome, Grab and bank BNPL only if they add them up. A consumer-held, real-time cross-provider exposure view is complementary to regulation, not a duplicate of it.

### Gaps
- No Malaysian or UK statistic on cross-provider stacking was found. The only quantified stacking figures are US.

## Q10. Which regulatory concepts translate into concrete, demoable features for a student-facing app?

### Takeaway
Six regulator-anchored features fit PaySense's existing engines and the "no fabricated numbers" rule, because each threshold or step is a cited regulatory constant and each value comes from the user's ledger or an engine. Ranked by citability for a Malaysian judging panel:
1. a cross-provider BNPL exposure meter against BNM's RM1,500 line;
2. a "lender-grade affordability receipt" mirroring the CCA/BNM check;
3. a missed-payment consequence simulator (CCA suspension rule, FCA missed-payment disclosure);
4. a credit-file impact explainer (CCRIS facility "Buy Now Pay Later");
5. an AKPK 3P pause-before-purchase gate;
6. hardship / AKPK referral triggered by ASIC-style vulnerability signals.

### Cited Findings (the regulatory hook for each feature)
- **Cross-provider exposure meter.** Hook: BNM's RM1,500 cumulative limit across all providers ([Skrine](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on)), the MOF "total commitments, bank and non-bank" test ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767)), and CFPB stacking evidence ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-research-reveals-heavy-buy-now-pay-later-use-among-borrowers-with-high-credit-balances-and-multiple-pay-in-four-loans/)).
- **Affordability assessment receipt.** Hook: the CCA requires creditworthiness and affordability before approval, with minimum inputs of repayment history and insolvency ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/790792); [HHQ](https://hhq.com.my/posts/how-to-conduct-a-bnpl-business-in-malaysia-under-the-cca-2025-top-10-key-takeaways/)). The BNM assessment covers verified income, overall indebtedness and a prudent DSR ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cc80cb96-54f3-403f-8f9c-3801131bcb20)). The UK applies CONC 5.2A proportionate assessment even under £50 ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/fca-confirms-final-rules-for-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-sector-or-deferred-payment-credit-ahead-of-new-regime/)). Australia uses an "unsuitability" test ([ASIC RG 281](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/2sgcg0y5/rg281-published-8-may-2025.pdf)). The EU requires a documented assessment with explainability ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d9e1b6e9-382e-4730-bd74-b1b85ad34296)).
- **Total-cost and key-facts card.** Hook: CCA ad rules (no "free" claims without clearly shown conditions; plain-language terms; transparent and reasonable fees) ([HHQ](https://hhq.com.my/posts/how-to-conduct-a-bnpl-business-in-malaysia-under-the-cca-2025-top-10-key-takeaways/); [Paydibs](https://paydibs.com/bnpl-new-regulation-2025-malaysia/)). FCA pre-contract information covers payment dates, amounts and missed-payment consequences ([HAT Group](https://hatgroup.co.uk/fca-regulation-of-deferred-payment-credit-from-15-july-2026/)).
- **Missed-payment consequence simulator.** Hook: under the CCA Conduct Standards an account is suspended after a reminder and up to two consecutive missed payments, until arrears and late charges are paid ([HHQ](https://hhq.com.my/posts/how-to-conduct-a-bnpl-business-in-malaysia-under-the-cca-2025-top-10-key-takeaways/)). The FCA requires prompt contact explaining the amount outstanding, fees and consequences ([SEON](https://seon.io/resources/buy-now-pay-later-uk-regulation-what-changed/)).
- **Credit-file impact explainer.** Hook: bank BNPL is reported to CCRIS as "Buy Now Pay Later" ([Skrine](http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-new-policy-document-on)). Officials have said BNPL data goes to credit reporting agencies such as CTOS ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/790792)), though this is contested ([malaysia4u](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)). The FCA requires disclosure of whether a CRA will or may be checked ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-fintech-and-digital-assets/buy-now-pay-later-the-final-furlong-policy-statement-ps26-1-regulation-of-deferred-payment-credit)). The EU gives a right to be told the database-check result ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj/eng)).
- **Pause-before-purchase (AKPK 3P) gate.** Hook: AKPK's 3P (Purpose, Payment capacity, Payment history) and its "do you really need that right now?" warning ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/748191); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/03/25/do-you-really-need-that-right-now-bnpl-can-be-useful-but-not-long-term-financial-solution-akpk-warns/170730)). The UK has a statutory withdrawal right under CCA s66A, signposted pre-contract ([FCA CP25/23](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp25-23.pdf); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/uk-bnpl-regime-fca-publishes-final-rules-102mkqc)).
- **Hardship / AKPK referral.** Hook: the CCA requires providers to consider hardship assistance ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/the-experts-corner/malaysias-new-consumer-credit-act-explained.html)). ASIC's vulnerability indicators include repeated late fees across products and substantial debt ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2d3b7578-4dea-4a02-8ab1-b4e74d1930b0)). Australian providers must follow NCC hardship provisions ([Law Council](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-05/c2024-504798-law-council-of-australia.pdf)).
- **Evidence for the pitch:** young Malaysians made 37.9% of BNPL transactions (RM5.4b) in H1 2026, with 4.0% of their RM2.4b outstanding non-performing ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767)).

### Inferences
- **Mapping to PaySense engines** (design suggestion, not sourced):
  - The "affordability receipt" can be Engine 1's simulated shortfall probability, plus Engine 2's risk class with SHAP reasons, plus the user's own commitments-to-income ratio. It would be laid out as the checklist a CCA/BNM lender should run (repayment history, existing commitments across providers, income, ability to repay in full) and could be exported as a "my own affordability check" receipt.
  - The exposure meter sums limits or outstanding amounts across providers from the ledger and draws BNM's RM1,500 line as a cited reference, not a PaySense-invented threshold.
  - The missed-payment simulator uses the provider rules served by `GET /api/providers`, plus the CCA two-missed-payments suspension rule once confirmed from the primary text.
- **Framing guardrail:** PaySense is not a licensed credit provider and cannot "approve" credit. Features should say "mirrors the check lenders must run" and never claim regulatory equivalence or compliance. The LLM layer (D-47) can narrate these checks but must not produce the numbers.
- **The strongest differentiator** is that the cross-provider view is the one thing regulators require of lenders but which neither lenders (data lag, partial bureau coverage of non-bank BNPL) nor consumers currently have in real time.
- **CLAUDE.md rule 7 note:** regulatory effective dates in these notes are records of law, not project deadlines. If they are copied into repo docs, keep them framed as facts about the regulation.

### Gaps
- No source confirms a Malaysian statutory cooling-off right for BNPL, so a "cooling-off timer" must be framed as an AKPK-style voluntary pause, not a legal right, in a Malaysian context.
- Before the demo uses the missed-payment suspension rule and the CCRIS/CTOS reporting position, both need confirming against SKP's primary text.
