## 1. BNPL usage statistics in Malaysia (users, value, growth, youth share, defaults, ticket size, purchases)

### Takeaway
BNPL in Malaysia is now mainstream and growing quickly. In 1H2026 there were **173.9 million transactions worth RM14.7 billion**, and **people aged 30 and under accounted for about 38% of transactions and 37% of value** (65.9 million transactions, RM5.4 billion). Under-30s held **RM2.4 billion outstanding, about 4% of it unpaid**. Tickets are small (around RM80–90) and mostly for everyday needs, not big-ticket items.

### Cited Findings
**Youth (≤30), 1H2026: Finance Ministry written parliamentary reply, published 13–14 Oct 2026 (corroborated by several outlets)**
- People aged 30 and below made **65.9 million BNPL transactions worth RM5.4 billion in 1H2026**. That is **37.9% of 173.9 million total transactions** and **36.7% of the RM14.7 billion total value**. — [The Star on X](https://x.com/staronline/status/2110221566583697531); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/14/malaysian-youths-rack-up-rm54bil-in-buy-now-pay-later-purchases); [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/14/finance-ministry-malaysians-under-30-rack-up-rm54b-in-bnpl-transactions-in-first-half-of-2026/239008); [Borneo Post](https://www.theborneopost.com/2026/10/14/finance-ministry-malaysians-under-30-rack-up-rm5-4-bln-in-bnpl-transactions-in-first-half-of-2026/)
- As of 30 June 2026, under-30s held **RM2.4 billion in outstanding BNPL financing, of which RM96.8 million (about 4%) was unpaid**. The ministry called this "still manageable". — [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/61480/payments-remittance-malaysia/bnpl-malaysia-youth-rm5-4-billion/); [The Rakyat Post, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.therakyatpost.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/16/malaysian-youths-make-4-in-10-bnpl-transactions-owe-rm2-4-billion/)
- The question was asked by Datuk Dr Alias Razak (PN–Kuala Nerus) about Consumer Credit Commission measures to curb over-indebtedness **across multiple BNPL platforms**. The Star says the ministry stressed affordability checks under the Consumer Credit Act 2025. The Rakyat Post says the reply did not detail specific measures. — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/14/malaysian-youths-rack-up-rm54bil-in-buy-now-pay-later-purchases); [The Rakyat Post](https://www.therakyatpost.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/16/malaysian-youths-make-4-in-10-bnpl-transactions-owe-rm2-4-billion/)
- The South China Morning Post framed the same figures as a "US$1.3bn short-term credit spree" that "sparks warning over bad debt". — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3370811/young-malaysians-us13bn-short-term-credit-spree-sparks-warning-over-bad-debt)

**National market, 2024–2026**
- In 2H2024 there were **83.8 million transactions worth RM7.1 billion**. In 1H2025 there were **102.6 million transactions worth RM9.3 billion** (+31% in value). — summarised in search results citing [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/52913/fintech-lending-malaysia/consumer-credit-bill-2025/) and [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/810918). The exact attribution between these two outlets is unverified.
- In 2H2025 there were **140.4 million transactions worth RM12.0 billion and 7.5 million active BNPL account holders**, from a Consumer Credit Oversight Board (CCOB) Task Force infographic. — [DFDL summary of CCA 2025](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/content-hub/articles/malaysia-a-summary-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025/). A consumer guide gives slightly different figures, 140.3 million transactions and RM11.9 billion: [malaysia4u BNPL guide](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide).
- In 2H2024 there were **5.1 million active BNPL accounts**. — secondary citation in [ResearchGate, youth bankruptcy paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391046687_Rising_Bankruptcy_Among_Malaysian_Youth_due_to_Behavioral_and_Financial_Mismanagement); the source text was truncated.
- **Full-year 2025: transactions up 66% to 243 million, and value up 78% to RM21.3 billion. Most transactions were for daily necessities (groceries, food, transport, services), with an average transaction value of RM91.** — search summary citing [The Edge, "Malaysians now owe over RM5 bil in BNPL loans — Finance Ministry"](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/810918). This is single-sourced, so verify it. It is consistent with 1H25 + 2H25 = 243.0 million transactions and RM21.3 billion.
- Outstanding BNPL was **RM2.8 billion (0.2% of household debt) at Dec 2024** ([Malay Mail, 24 Mar 2025, BNM FSR 2H24](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/2025/03/24/bnpl-debt-reaches-rm28b-in-malaysia-as-users-grow-but-bank-negara-says-risks-are-contained/170685)). It was **RM4.9 billion (about 0.3%) at Dec 2025, with 3.3–3.4% overdue** (sources differ), according to the same search summary citing [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/810918). The Edge headline says "over RM5 bil".
- **BNM Q1 2026: outstanding RM5.3 billion, with RM181 million (3.4%) overdue.** — search summary citing [amanahkredit.my](https://amanahkredit.my/en/blog/perangkap-bnpl), a secondary source.
- **Earlier warning sign:** in BNM's FSR 2H2022, the share of BNPL users with overdue payments rose to **17% in 4Q22 from 7% in 4Q21**. BNM said users skew **younger and lower-income** and flagged conduct risk from unclear late fees. — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnm-sees-growing-trend-of-missed-repayments-among-bnpl-borrowers.html). Note that this measures the *share of users* overdue, while the 3–4% figures above are the *share of balances* overdue. The two are not comparable.
- **CCOB repayment survey (about 21,000 users): 88% paid on time, about 12% had overdue payments but could still repay, and under 1% could not meet obligations.** — search summary of [The Rakyat Post](https://www.therakyatpost.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/16/malaysian-youths-make-4-in-10-bnpl-transactions-owe-rm2-4-billion/) / [amanahkredit.my](https://amanahkredit.my/en/blog/perangkap-bnpl). The survey date and method are unknown.
- **Historic youth share:** about 5 million BNPL users in 2022, of whom **about 44% (about 2.2 million) were aged 18–30**. — secondary citation in academic papers ([ResearchGate, Gen Z BNPL](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391774840_Buy_Now_Pay_Later_Understanding_BNPL_Trends_Among_Generation_Z)); the original source is not verified.
- **Provider popularity (convenience sample):** one academic survey reports **Shopee PayLater is the most popular BNPL service (47.4%)**, but the sample base is unclear. — [ResearchGate, social media and BNPL adoption](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384219827_The_Impact_of_Social_Media_on_BNPL_Adoption_in_Malaysia)

### Inferences
- **Average youth ticket ≈ RM82** (RM5.4 billion ÷ 65.9 million) against **≈ RM85 nationally** in 1H2026 (RM14.7 billion ÷ 173.9 million), and RM91 for full-year 2025. These are my own calculations. BNPL in Malaysia is mostly **small, frequent, everyday spending** (food, groceries, transport), not occasional phone purchases. That makes **stacking many small plans** the real risk, not one big plan.
- National volume grew about 24% half-on-half (2H25 → 1H26: 140.4 million → 173.9 million), and value about 22% (RM12.0 billion → RM14.7 billion). These are my own calculations from the figures above.
- Under-30s owe RM2.4 billion out of a national total of about RM5.3 billion, so **roughly 45% of outstanding BNPL balances sit with under-30s**. That is more than their 37% share of value, which suggests they carry balances longer or use longer tenors. This is an inference, not a reported statistic.
- A 4% *balance* overdue rate can coexist with a much higher *share of users* who have missed at least one payment (17% in 2022; 12% in the CCOB survey). The latter is the more relevant figure for a student-facing app.

### Gaps
- There is no official breakdown for **students specifically**, as opposed to people aged 30 and under.
- There is no official purchase-category breakdown by age. The "daily necessities" description is national and single-sourced.
- No provider-funded Malaysian surveys (Atome, Shopee, Rakuten Insight, iPrice, YouGov) with youth usage percentages turned up.
- The latest BNM FSR (1H2026) text on BNPL was not retrieved.

## 2. Providers active in Malaysia and their terms (2025–2026); regulation and credit reporting

### Takeaway
The market is led by **Shopee SPayLater, Atome and Grab PayLater**, plus Boost PayFlex (Shariah, works at DuitNow QR merchants), Split, ShopBack PayLater and TikTok Shop PayLater. Pay-in-3 is usually 0%. Longer tenors (6–24 months) typically cost **about 1.5% per month**. Late fees are flat (about RM10 at SPayLater and Grab, up to about RM23–30 at Atome). The **Consumer Credit Act 2025 has been in force since 1 Mar 2026**, and BNPL licensing has applied from 1 Jun 2026 with a transition period to 31 Dec 2026. **Whether BNPL repayment history reaches CCRIS or CTOS is contested.**

### Cited Findings
**Terms by provider** (from consumer guides; verify against current T&Cs)
- **Atome:** pay-in-3 at 0%. 6-, 9- and 12-month plans charge **1.5% of order amount per month** at selected merchants. Merchant count is quoted as 5,000+ or 8,000+ (sources conflict). — [StashAway, Best BNPL Malaysia 2026](https://www.stashaway.my/r/best-buy-now-pay-later-malaysia); [malaysia4u Atome review](https://malaysia4u.com/review/atome)
- **Atome late fees conflict:** RM23 at selected merchants and RM30 elsewhere, plus RM7 "in certain cases"; or RM30 capped at RM60 per purchase; or up to RM23 per overdue instalment (2026 guide). — [malaysia4u Atome review](https://malaysia4u.com/review/atome); [ScienceOpen preprint, BNPL vs EPP fine print](https://www.scienceopen.com/document_file/8ae1106b-3b46-4a23-9799-69e8b5e8f6e8/ScienceOpenPreprint/Transparency%20in%20the%20Fine%20Print%20of%20BNPL%20vs%20EPP%20in%20Malaysia%20Context%20-%20Who%20Really%20Protects%20the%20Consumer.pdf)
- **Shopee SPayLater:** **1 to 24-month plans**. One month is free. Qualifying three-month Shopee Marketplace purchases are 0%. Most other plans charge **1.5% per month**. The late charge is **RM10 or the principal bill, whichever is lower**, and the account is **frozen until settled**. — [StashAway](https://www.stashaway.my/r/best-buy-now-pay-later-malaysia); [malaysia4u BNPL guide](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)
- **Grab PayLater:** Grab's page describes a single **flat RM10** late charge to reactivate a suspended account. — [Grab MY PayLater](https://www.grab.com/my/finance/pay-later/). An older RinggitPlus article gives **RM10 per missed instalment, capped at RM30**, and says Grab **checks CTOS at activation but does not report payment history**. — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/the-experts-corner/how-grab-paylater-works-in-malaysia.html)
- **Boost PayFlex:** Shariah-compliant. **Up to 24-month instalments**. Accepted at **1.8 million+ DuitNow QR merchants**. It reportedly charges **1% p.a. on overdue amounts**; whether there is a separate wakalah fee is unclear. — [malaysia4u Boost PayFlex review](https://malaysia4u.com/review/boost-payflex); [StashAway](https://www.stashaway.my/r/best-buy-now-pay-later-malaysia)
- **Split:** 0% over 2–3 instalments. Described as a "homegrown Shariah-compliant" provider, and elsewhere as "Split by Maybank". The descriptions conflict. — [StashAway](https://www.stashaway.my/r/best-buy-now-pay-later-malaysia); [Rankpage Top 10](https://www.rankpage.com.my/fintech/top-10-bnpl-providers-in-malaysia/)
- **Indicative limits:** Atome RM1,000–10,000 and Grab PayLater RM500–5,000, from a single unofficial source. — [beautysachet comparison](https://www.beautysachet.com/top-buy-now-pay-later-malaysia/)
- Fintech News Malaysia names Atome, SPayLater and Grab PayLater as the top three. — [Fintech News Malaysia, Top 3 BNPLs](https://fintechnews.my/top-3-bnpls-in-malaysia-and-whats-next-for-the-market/)

**Regulation**
- The **Consumer Credit Act 2025** was gazetted on **31 Dec 2025** and came into force on **1 Mar 2026**. It establishes the **Consumer Credit Commission (Suruhanjaya Kredit Pengguna)**. **BNPL companies must be licensed**, with licensing from **1 Jun 2026** and a transition period to **31 Dec 2026**. — [CCOB](https://ccob.my/enforcement-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025-and-the-establishment-of-the-consumer-credit-commission/); [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/794624); [DFDL](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/content-hub/articles/malaysia-a-summary-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025/)
- Providers must carry out **creditworthiness and affordability assessments** before approving financing (Deputy Finance Minister). — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/790792); [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnpl-providers-required-to-run-credit-checks-under-consumer-credit-act-2025.html)
- RAM Ratings called the CCA "credit positive". — [RAM](https://www.ram.com.my/pressrelease/?prviewid=7068)

**Credit reporting (CCRIS/CTOS): contested**
- 2025 coverage of the bill said BNPL providers would have to report data to agencies such as CTOS through a centralised data platform. — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/748795)
- A 2026 consumer guide says the CCA has **no credit-reporting provisions** and that BNPL generally **does not appear in CCRIS or CTOS**, although providers may subscribe to private bureaus voluntarily. — [malaysia4u BNPL guide](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)
- Another 2026 article claims late BNPL payments are now reported to CTOS and CCRIS, but it gives no statutory citation. — [amanahkredit.my](https://amanahkredit.my/en/blog/perangkap-bnpl)

### Inferences
- Malaysia has **no cross-provider view of a person's BNPL exposure**. The ministry was asked precisely about over-indebtedness "across multiple BNPL platforms". An app that **aggregates every plan a student has, across all providers, into one cash-flow calendar** fills a gap that regulation has not yet closed.
- Fee structures differ on tenor (0% for pay-in-3, 1.5% per month for longer) and late fees range from RM10 to RM30. A tool that **converts each offer into an effective cost in sen** and compares tenors is directly useful.

### Gaps
- No primary information was found on **TikTok Shop PayLater** or **ShopBack PayLater** terms.
- No official BNPL credit-limit ranges per provider.
- The definitive CCRIS/CTOS reporting position needs the CCA text or a Consumer Credit Commission circular.
- The list of providers licensed by the Consumer Credit Commission was not found.

## 3. Student income patterns (PTPTN, allowances, scholarships, part-time work, cost of living)

### Takeaway
Student income is **lumpy**. PTPTN loans are set as annual amounts (for example, up to **RM6,650 per year** for a public-university science bachelor's on the official table) and are paid **once per semester** into the student's account, after takaful/WPP deductions. Spending is monthly and estimated at **RM800–1,500 per month** for hostel-based students at public universities. No source directly measured "lump-sum income causes BNPL use".

### Cited Findings
- The official PTPTN table lists **yearly** loan amounts by level. Public-university **Bachelor's (Science) maximum is RM6,650**, with 75% and 50% tiers of **RM4,990 and RM3,330**. Amounts received each semester are **net of takaful and WPP deductions**. — [PTPTN official portal](https://www.ptptn.gov.my/en/pinjaman-pendidikan/)
- Disbursement is **once per semester** into the student's personal bank account ([RinggitPlus PTPTN 2026](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/career/funding-your-education-ptptn-101.html)), or "twice per academic year, once per long semester" ([Unienrol](https://articles.unienrol.com/how-to-apply-for-ptptn-a-step-by-step-guide/)). Unienrol quotes much higher degree maxima (RM13,600 for business/arts and RM14,030 for science/engineering), which conflict with the official table and may refer to private universities.
- At private institutions (IPTS) the loan may not include a living allowance. — [PTPTN loan guide PDF (MAHSA)](https://storage.googleapis.com/msgsndr/AOMyulmuEE36EZvxn9Zz/media/685897924e7e8133851fde1f.pdf); [Press.com.my](https://www.press.com.my/education/guide-to-ptptn-loan-in-malaysia/)
- **Cost of living (commercial estimate, not a survey):** RM800–1,500 per month in general. IPTA hostel residents need about RM800–1,000, and off-campus renters in KL about RM1,500–2,500. Indicative part-time income is RM600–1,500 per month for tutoring and RM300–600 for campus jobs. — [studiesinmalaysia.com](https://www.studiesinmalaysia.com/blogs/study/student-budget-in-malaysia-per-month-2026); [calculatormalaysia.com](https://calculatormalaysia.com/calculator/student-monthly-budget-calculator-malaysia/)
- **Academic surveys:**
  - UUM 2024: monthly student expenditure correlates with age, gender, ethnicity, year of study, income, financial knowledge and **peer influence**. Expenditure bands ran from ≤RM200 to ≥RM801. — [UUM IJBF 2024](https://repo.uum.edu.my/30370/1/IJBF%2019%2001%202024%20107-132.pdf)
  - 2025 study, n=480 undergraduates: some students reduce financial dependence through part-time work or entrepreneurship. — [IJRISS vol 9 issue 9](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/Digital-Library/volume-9-issue-9/6729-6740.pdf)
  - UniMAP 2026, n=200 public-university students: monthly allowance shows only a weak positive correlation with academic performance. — [UniMAP IJBT](https://ejournal.unimap.edu.my/index.php/ijbt/article/download/2485/1609/13107)
- **Spending structure (SC youth survey):** most youth income goes on food, household expenses and **debt repayment**, which leaves little for savings. — [iMoney on SC survey](https://www.imoney.my/articles/securities-commission-survey)

### Inferences
- With PTPTN, the typical shape is roughly **RM3,000+ arriving once per semester** (half of RM6,650 at the full tier, minus deductions) against spending of **RM800–1,000+ per month**. That is about 4–5 months of runway from one lump sum, a classic **"rich in week 1, broke in month 4"** cash-flow profile. BNPL due dates that fall **before the next disbursement** are the high-risk zone. This is an inference: no Malaysian study found here tests the PTPTN-timing → BNPL-use link directly.
- A cash-flow forecast that **models semester lump sums, monthly allowances and irregular gig income separately** (rather than a flat monthly salary) fits Malaysian students better than generic budgeting apps do.

### Gaps
- No official or large-sample survey of **average student allowance or monthly spend** (for example from UM, UiTM or UTP) was found.
- MARA, JPA and state scholarship allowance amounts and payment frequency were not retrieved.
- No data on student gig-economy participation (Grab, Foodpanda riders) was found.
- No study directly links the timing of PTPTN or allowance payments to BNPL uptake.

## 4. Harms: youth debt, bankruptcy, loan stacking, credit-record impact

### Takeaway
Harm among young people is real but mostly shows up **downstream**. AKPK reports **53,000 under-30s holding about RM1.9 billion in debt** (2024), with **shopping addiction as the top cause**. Youth bankruptcies reached **877 in 2024 (+150 year on year)** and **5,272 under-34s were declared bankrupt from 2020 to early 2025**. Total bankruptcies rose **13.4% to 6,776 in 2025**. BNPL is rarely the direct cause of bankruptcy (personal loans dominate), but it feeds the habits and multi-plan exposure that precede it.

### Cited Findings
- **AKPK (2024):** **53,000 individuals under 30 carry nearly RM1.9 billion** in cumulative debt. AKPK data also shows **28% of working adults borrowed to buy essentials**. — [Malay Mail, 1 Oct 2024](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/10/01/under-30-and-drowning-in-debt-53000-young-adults-in-malaysia-owe-rm19b-as-credit-culture-soars/152230)
- **AKPK survey:** **shopping addiction is the top reason** young people fall into serious debt, and **65% of those seeking AKPK help are aged 28–30**. — [Malay Mail, 18 Jun 2024](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/06/18/credit-counselling-agency-finds-shopping-addiction-top-reason-young-malaysians-in-debt/140271)
- **Youth bankruptcy (Youth Minister Hannah Yeoh, Dewan Negara, Mar 2025):** **5,272 people under 34 declared bankrupt from 2020 to early 2025** (5,189 aged 25–34 and 83 under 25), and **877 cases in 2024, up 150 on 2023**. — [Malay Mail, 13 Mar 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/03/13/bankruptcy-among-youths-on-the-rise-877-cases-recorded-in-2024-more-than-5000-since-2020-says-hannah-yeoh/169459); [Syok](https://syok.my/en/news/young-malaysians-declared-bankrupt-until-2025)
- **All bankruptcies:** **5,977 in 2024**, with personal loans the largest cause at **2,776 cases (46.4%)**. **6,776 in 2025 (+13.4%)**. **4,301 in Jan–Jul 2026** against 3,679 in the same period of 2025, with people aged 35–54 making up 68.2%. The bankruptcy threshold will rise **from RM100,000 to RM150,000**. — [The Edge, Oct 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821292)
- The government aimed to discharge 200,000 people from bankruptcy in 2025. — [The Star, 23 Jun 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2025/06/23/govt-aims-for-200000-individuals-to-be-discharged-from-bankruptcy-in-2025)
- One academic paper says about 59% of bankruptcy cases from 2018 to 2024 involved "young adults", using a different age definition. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391046687_Rising_Bankruptcy_Among_Malaysian_Youth_due_to_Behavioral_and_Financial_Mismanagement)
- **Loan stacking** is the policy concern: the parliamentary question was about over-indebtedness **across multiple BNPL platforms**. — [The Rakyat Post](https://www.therakyatpost.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/16/malaysian-youths-make-4-in-10-bnpl-transactions-owe-rm2-4-billion/)
- **Students, n=366 (Peninsular Malaysia, Sep 2025):** most were aware of the bankruptcy risk from BNPL, and about half knew the bankruptcy threshold. **Yet a significant share still had outstanding payments or used BNPL despite unstable finances.** — [AIJBES 7(25), 2025](https://gaexcellence.com/aijbes/article/download/5819/5336/19644)
- **Frictionless design → impulse buying** among Borneo (Sabah and Sarawak) university students using SPayLater, Grab PayLater and Atome (working paper). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6241078)
- **Account freeze:** SPayLater freezes the account until arrears are settled. — [malaysia4u](https://malaysia4u.com/bnpl-guide)
- BFM podcast: youth debt across BNPL, credit cards and loans, featuring AKPK and BNM perspectives. — [BFM](https://www.bfm.my/content/podcast/youth-debt-malaysia-bnpl-credit-card-akpk-bnm-loan)

### Inferences
- The awareness–behaviour gap is the key finding for product design. Students **know** the risks (the n=366 study) but **still use BNPL while finances are unstable**. Education alone is not enough. What helps is a **just-in-time check at the point of purchase**, such as "this plan's third instalment lands 2 weeks after your money runs out". That is what PaySense's forecast + risk engine pair provides.
- AKPK's caseload peaks at ages 28–30, years after university. The damage from student-era habits **surfaces later**, which supports a preventive, student-stage tool.
- **CCRIS and job impact:** credit reporting of BNPL is contested (section 2). Claims that BNPL defaults hurt future loans or jobs should be stated **conditionally** on a slide.

### Gaps
- No verified count of **students with multiple simultaneous BNPL plans**.
- No 2024–2026 news case studies of individual students were retrieved, because article fetches were blocked.
- No MdI dataset broken down by age was accessed directly; youth figures come from ministerial statements.
- No evidence was found on employers checking CTOS/CCRIS for graduate hires.

## 5. Youth financial literacy and language preferences

### Takeaway
Malaysian financial **knowledge is improving while behaviour is getting worse**. In BNM's FCI 2024, knowledge rose from 60.2 to 62.9 while behaviour fell from 61.9 to 59.9. **61% could not raise RM1,000 in an emergency** (47% in 2021). Young people report higher debt burdens from student loans and store credit. Youth prefer **social media and web channels** for learning. No source on youth language preference (BM, English, Chinese, Tamil) for finance apps was found.

### Cited Findings
- **BNM Financial Capability & Inclusion Demand Side Survey 2024:** knowledge score went from **60.2 (2021) to 62.9 (2024)**; behaviour score from **61.9 to 59.9**. **61% have difficulty raising RM1,000 for an emergency** (2021: 47%). **26% feel they carry too much debt** (30% in 2021), and **12% are highly indebted** (14%). The majority of the highly indebted are **young people struggling with student loans, store credit and debts to family and friends**. — [BNM Annual Report 2024, Box 4](https://alumni.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124/17493532/ar2024_en_box4.pdf)
- **National Strategy for Financial Literacy 2026–2030 (FEN):** youth report a higher debt burden, mainly from student loans, store credit and informal borrowing. — [FEN NS2](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf)
- **SC youth survey:** youth **prefer learning finance via social media and websites** over traditional methods, and most income goes to food, household expenses and debt. — [iMoney](https://www.imoney.my/articles/securities-commission-survey)

### Inferences
- "Knowledge up, behaviour down" is the same awareness–behaviour gap seen in section 4. It argues for **behavioural nudges and forecasts over lessons**.
- 61% cannot raise RM1,000 in an emergency. That supports the optional **savings buffer** feature (D-50) as a nationally relevant default, not an edge case.

### Gaps
- FCI 2024 age-band tables (18–30) were not retrieved.
- No OECD/INFE Malaysia youth score was retrieved.
- **No source on language preference** for financial apps among Malaysian students. As a working assumption, BM and English are the minimum and Chinese is a likely third language, but this is not evidenced here.

## 6. What Malaysian students say they need (academic studies, intention models)

### Takeaway
Malaysian BNPL research is mostly **small convenience-sample surveys (n≈118–480)** using TAM, UTAUT or TPB. They consistently find that **perceived usefulness, social influence and attitude** drive BNPL intention and that BNPL is linked to **impulse buying**. Few studies ask students what support they want. The implied need is **friction at the right moment, and visibility of total commitments**.

### Cited Findings
- **Young adults, n=118, UTAUT + SEM:** **perceived usefulness and social influence** significantly increase BNPL intention (Soong et al., 2024). — [HRMARS PDF](https://hrmars.com/papers_submitted/23789/the-intention-of-young-adults-in-malaysia-toward-using-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-in-an-online-shopping-environment.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387404104_The_Intention_of_Young_Adults_in_Malaysia_toward_Using_Buy_Now_Pay_Later_BNPL_in_an_Online_Shopping_Environment)
- **TPB applied to BNPL intention, Seremban, n=384 aged 20+** (UiTM Journal of Administrative Science). — [UiTM JAS](https://journal.uitm.edu.my/index.php/JAS/article/view/10784)
- **Gen Z in Shah Alam, n=350 BNPL users (41% students):** studies BNPL's effect on impulsive buying. — [IJRISS](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/articles/the-impact-of-buy-now-pay-later-services-on-the-impulsive-buying-behavior-of-generation-z-in-shah-alam-malaysia/)
- **University students, n=150 active BNPL users:** BNPL usage and debt management (2025/26). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401246325_Buy_Now_Pay_Later_BNPL_Usage_and_Its_Impact_on_Debt_Management_Among_Malaysian_University_Students)
- **Students, n=366:** high risk awareness coexists with continued use under financial instability. — [AIJBES](https://gaexcellence.com/aijbes/view/5819)
- **Millennials and Gen Z intention factors** — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/17305527). **BNPL and Gen Z spending** — [IMBR](https://ojs.amhinternational.com/index.php/imbr/article/view/4092). **Social media drives BNPL adoption** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384219827_The_Impact_of_Social_Media_on_BNPL_Adoption_in_Malaysia). **Impacts among Millennials and Gen Z** — [IJRISS](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/articles/exploring-the-impacts-of-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-payment-method-among-millennials-and-generation-z-in-malaysia/)

### Inferences: pain points a decision-support app could solve
Each pain point is mapped to the evidence above.
1. **No view of total BNPL exposure across providers.** The parliamentary concern about multi-platform over-indebtedness, the lack of confirmed bureau reporting, and 7.5 million active account holders all point here. → A unified commitment calendar across providers.
2. **Instalments collide with lumpy income** (semester PTPTN or allowances, irregular part-time pay). → A forecast that projects shortfall dates against when money actually arrives.
3. **Small everyday tickets of about RM80–90 add up unnoticed.** → Cumulative "plan stacking" alerts rather than per-purchase checks.
4. **Awareness without behaviour change** (n=366 study; FCI knowledge up, behaviour down). → A just-in-time, explainable risk check at the point of decision, with SHAP-style reasons.
5. **Opaque and heterogeneous fees** (0% vs 1.5% per month; late fees RM10–30; account freezes). → An effective-cost comparison of tenors and providers in plain BM or English.
6. **Thin emergency buffers** (61% cannot raise RM1,000). → Buffer-aware affordability (D-50).
7. **Social influence and social-media channels drive uptake and learning** (UTAUT study; SC survey). → Short shareable nudges. Distribution potentially through campus or social channels.

### Gaps
- No Malaysian study was found that asks students directly which **features or tools** they want from a BNPL decision aid, from focus groups or co-design.
- No 2024–2025 TPB study specifically on Malaysian **university students** and BNPL was confirmed.
- Most samples are small, non-random and single-campus, so they are not nationally representative. Sponsors are academic, with no provider funding identified.
