# Show students what this purchase adds

PaySense's strongest edge for FinTechathon 2026 is not a new chatbot skill or another security layer. It is a sharpened version of the risk check it already runs. Call it the **Second-Opinion Check**. For one pending BNPL purchase, it shows the student's chance of running short **before** the purchase, **after** it, the **points this purchase adds**, the stacked instalments across every provider that cause the change, and the **earliest date and tenor at which the purchase stops adding meaningful risk**. It is framed as the affordability check that Malaysia's Consumer Credit Act now requires of lenders but that no lender shows the borrower. Every visible Topic A rival already claims the "model proposes, deterministic code decides" safety pattern, and none shows a benchmarked, probabilistic, cross-provider answer to "how much worse does *this* purchase make my position?". That question is also where behavioural evidence says disclosure changes behaviour, and where the engine evaluation says PaySense's current output is weakest. The API already returns both the before and the after number, but the frontend shows only the after one, so a first version can be built now in a cloud session. The full version needs the author's sign-off on four engine changes: per-month clipping in the simulation, a "risk before plus added risk" target for Engine 2, a counterfactual objective for students who are already short, and a generator that includes short histories. Without those changes the demo persona would honestly read "100% → 100%". Two supporting moves come second. The first is an on-screen judge mode that maps the existing attack suite to the four named attack classes, because 30% of the score is security and rivals already show scoreboards. The second is a synthetic outcome check that turns "measurable value" into a benchmarked number.

## Rivals already own the safety story; nobody owns the decision

The rubric rewards completion first and punishes visible failure hardest. The International Track scores **task completion 40%, security and compliance 30%, innovation and interaction 30%**, with a capped 0–5 bonus and a separate **Best Innovation Award (RMB 20,000)** ([Appendix 2 rules](https://github.com/beingmushfiq/CenterPoint-ProERP/blob/HEAD/PBCSF%20Appendix%202-Problem%20Description%20and%20Competition%20Rules%20for%20International%20Track.md)). Two independent teams transcribed the website's sub-criteria. Security is judged against **induced transfers, data extraction, privilege escalation and prompt injection**, with "serious security failures trigger substantial deductions", and interaction is judged on "novelty, real-world value, dialogue efficiency, clarification, correction and fallback" ([leash SUBMISSION.md](https://github.com/Brieuclerouxtardif/leash/blob/HEAD/docs/SUBMISSION.md); [guardian Guardian.md](https://github.com/ahammadshawki8/guardian/blob/HEAD/Guardian.md)). The panel is "WeBank industry experts and university scholars", and FinTechathon's past AI-track first prizes went to technically deep work, often federated learning on WeBank's FATE platform ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.cn/article/njklsrobg4rfmaaydu13); [SZU news](https://www.szu.edu.cn/info/1161/18428.htm)). The 2025 champion was a multi-agent system that ran a full workflow from data to execution ([Dutenews](https://m.dutenews.com/n/article/10315760)). Bankers and academics reward rigour and an end-to-end task more than polish.

The visible 2026 field shows what no longer differentiates. **FundBun** (Topic A) claims "45/45 scripted scenarios · 24/24 attacks blocked (¥0 moved)", a five-tier permission engine, a "Glass Box" trace panel, a "Should I buy it?" check priced in hours of work, and a hosted demo ([fundbun README](https://github.com/odripads/fundbun)). **Monetra AI** has PaySense's own architecture: FastAPI with deterministic services, Gemini explaining verified values, and a read-only what-if purchase simulator ([monetra-ai](https://github.com/RR0327/monetra-ai)). **DialectGuard**, another Malaysian Topic A entry, keeps the AI off the payment path and signs approvals ([Teman PROPOSAL.md](https://github.com/25006675-png/Teman/blob/HEAD/PROPOSAL.md)). Adjacent entrants add an in-app "Judge mode" that runs 20 of 20 tasks ([finbuddy](https://github.com/Tasrif-Ahmed-Mohsin/finbuddy)) and a red-team scoreboard that separates "AI fooled" from "money lost $0" ([guardian](https://github.com/ahammadshawki8/guardian/blob/HEAD/Guardian.md)). Permission tiers, confirm-before-write, injection tests and audit logs are therefore **table stakes**. PaySense has all of them, so they protect its score but do not lift it above FundBun's.

The open gap is the decision itself. Commercial assistants already do chat over transactions, insights, voice and a short balance projection: Erica projects seven days ahead ([BofA](https://newsroom.bankofamerica.com/content/newsroom/press-releases/2025/08/a-decade-of-ai-innovation--bofa-s-virtual-assistant-erica-surpas.html)), and Ryt AI handles Manglish while its bank sells PayLater credit of up to RM1,499 ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/bank-news/ryt-bank-launches-as-malaysias-first-ai-powered-bank.html)). None of the products found answers whether a student can afford *this* plan, across *all* their providers, *before* checkout, with reasons. The provider-side assistants profit from the purchase ([OpenAI on Klarna](https://openai.com/customer-stories/klarna)). The policy context sharpens the gap. Malaysia's Consumer Credit Act 2025 requires BNPL providers to assess creditworthiness and affordability ([SKP](https://www.skp.gov.my/en/news/media-releases/enforcement-of-the-consumer-credit-act-2025-and-the-establishment-of-the-consumer-credit-commission); [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/790792)). The Finance Ministry's latest reply says that check must cover **"total commitments, including bank and non-bank credit"**, and that under-30s made **37.9% of BNPL transactions (RM5.4b) in H1 2026, with 4.0% of their RM2.4b outstanding non-performing** ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821767)). Each lender sees only its own application, so the cross-provider, forward-looking view is exactly what nobody hands the borrower.

## Four rounds of debate narrow nine candidates to one

### Round one puts nine candidates on the table

The research notes yield nine candidates:

1. **A cross-provider exposure meter** drawn against BNM's RM1,500 cumulative-limit line.
2. **A before-and-after "added risk" card** for one pending purchase.
3. **A "make it safe" planner**: the smallest change, or the best date and tenor.
4. **A judge mode with an attack scoreboard and a glass-box trace.**
5. **Measured value**: an outcome check and a policy replay, with and without PaySense.
6. **A checkout entry point** from a pasted cart or a screenshot.
7. **A federated or privacy-preserving nod** to WeBank's FATE.
8. **A cooling-off or AKPK "3P" pause**, delivered as a reminder.
9. **Multilingual explanations** in BM and Chinese.

### Round three merges the survivors into one flow

Candidates 2, 3 and 8, plus a line from candidate 1, are one task, not four features. That matters, because rival advice drawn from the rubric is to "show one complete workflow rather than many disconnected features" ([Monara research](https://github.com/ZoeMohamed/Monara/blob/HEAD/deck/research/sea-market-competition-compliance.md)). The merged **Second-Opinion Check** runs in five steps:

1. The student asks about a purchase in chat or on the risk screen. The assistant asks for any missing field (clarification).
2. The card shows the student's simulated chance of running short **without** the purchase, **with** it, and the **added points**.
3. A dues timeline adds up every unpaid instalment across SPayLater, Atome, Grab and the new plan against forecast income dates (adding-up salience).
4. Shapley reasons explain the *added* risk, not the starting position.
5. A scheduler proposes the earliest confirmed income date and tenor at which the purchase adds under a pre-registered threshold. The LLM narrates the proposal and offers a pending action that the student confirms on the existing digest-checked confirm card. Today that is the existing `create_plan` action with the proposed date and tenor. A new "remind me on allowance day" action kind would be a second, separately tested write.

Candidates 4 and 5 become the two proofs behind the flow: one that it is safe, one that it is right.

The behavioural evidence favours this shape over a risk label. In Bertrand and Morse's field experiment, the treatment that **showed the adding-up of dollar fees** cut payday borrowing **by about 11% over four months** ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/information-disclosure-cognitive-biases-and-payday-borrowing-united-states)). Salient BNPL risk disclosures in the Central Bank of Ireland's randomised experiment **improved understanding but did not change usage**. The same study found that prior BNPL use made a discretionary purchase **22.2% more likely** by inflating perceived funds ([CBI RTP 15/2025](https://www.centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/publications/research-technical-papers/rtp-15-2025-behavioural-mechanisms-of-bnpl.pdf?sfvrsn=9b816e1a_8)). The lesson for the card is to make the student's own stacked money concrete against their own forecast, and to avoid generic warnings. Malaysian students fit this pattern. In a survey of 366 students, most knew BNPL's bankruptcy risk yet still used it while their finances were unstable ([AIJBES](https://gaexcellence.com/aijbes/article/download/5819/5336/19644)). BNM's survey shows financial knowledge rising from 60.2 to 62.9 while behaviour fell from 61.9 to 59.9 ([BNM AR 2024 Box 4](https://alumni.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124/17493532/ar2024_en_box4.pdf)). Both point to a just-in-time check over another lesson. The scheduler suits the cash-flow shape. PTPTN arrives **once per semester** ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/career/funding-your-education-ptptn-101.html)), against yearly maxima of RM6,650 for a public science degree ([PTPTN](https://www.ptptn.gov.my/en/pinjaman-pendidikan/)). Whether an instalment lands before or after the next disbursement is the lever a student actually controls.

### Round four stress-tests the winner against the judges' hard questions

**"Why AI here?"** The LLM handles the parts that need language: clarifying missing fields, correcting a wrong provider, narrating the reasons, and proposing the scheduled action. Every number comes from Engine 1, Engine 2 or the ledger, and the existing number check rejects any figure not found in tool results. That property matters because general LLMs get about **30% of personal-finance questions wrong** ([arXiv 2412.19784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19784v1)).

**"Isn't this Monetra's what-if or FundBun's 'Should I buy it?'"** Both are deterministic affordability arithmetic for one purchase. PaySense gives a calibrated probability from a benchmarked engine, across providers, as a marginal change, and returns a date.

**"Your numbers are synthetic."** The outcome check scores P(shortfall) against realised synthetic outcomes, with a constant benchmark and an MA13 benchmark. The pre-registered vignette study is the route to human evidence. Its expected effect should be stated as small, because nudge effects at scale average **1.4 pp against 8.7 pp in journals** ([DellaVigna & Linos](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27594)).

**"Is this credit advice?"** No. It is decision support that "mirrors the check lenders must run", never "approves". It says nothing about compliance on PaySense's own behalf.

The one failure this round exposes is the demo. If the engine fixes are not signed off, the headline persona shows no added risk. So the demo must either use a persona and purchase pair where the simulated difference is visible, chosen by re-running `find_demo_scenario` and reported honestly, or wait for the bundled retrain.

## The engines answer the wrong question for already-short students

The engine evaluation's central finding is that **Engine 2 is very good at reproducing a student's starting position and weaker on the purchase**. On all 10,000 labels, predicting the label as the simulated no-purchase probability explains **71% of label variance** (Brier 0.0484 against 0.1680 for a constant). The purchase moves the band for only **10.7%** of students, and with a shared seed it can never lower risk ([engine note R3–R5](../research_notes/PaySense%20hackathon%20edge%20feature/engine_evaluation.md); [Engine 2 report](../../../../reports/engine2/README.md)). In cross-validation on development rows, the current LightGBM puts **73.6%** of the cases where the purchase moves the band into the right band, against **91.3% overall**. It is weakest where the decision matters. Shapley reasons computed against a Safe reference therefore mostly explain why the student is already short. The counterfactual can bring only 14 of 111 non-Safe results to Safe ([serving check](../../../../reports/engine2/serving_check.md)). One more fact settles the classifier's role: serving already simulates every check (p50 0.12 s). Its value is explanation (Shapley values on a smooth surrogate) and the FYP objective, not accuracy over the simulation it imitates.

Engine 1 has three measured defects that flow straight into those labels. First, the 80% band covers the 13-week total only **0.69 (spending) and 0.53 (income)** held out, and **0.38 / 0.33 for 17–25-week histories** ([band calibration](../../../../reports/engine1/band_calibration.md); [fresh check](../../../../reports/engine1/band_fresh_check.md)). Second, the exploratory pass traced most of the income bias and some of the under-coverage to `simulate.py` **clipping every simulated week at zero**. In that pass, clipping per month or on the total brought median bias to about zero and coverage to **0.77–0.83** after the table was refitted. Third, the income network and its band were fitted on total income, while serving forecasts only the residual income left after recurring items are confirmed: a median 0% of income, with 81% zero weeks. Two further defects compound this. Engine 2 trains only on **73–78-week** histories while the personas have about **21 weeks**, and the recursive LSTM drifts to **−0.338 cumulative error at 52 weeks**, against −0.047 for MA13 ([engine note N1–N5](../research_notes/PaySense%20hackathon%20edge%20feature/engine_evaluation.md)). These N-series numbers come from throwaway scripts on development students. They are **exploratory and must not appear in the deck** until a committed `ml.` script reproduces them.

The defects and the headline feature meet in one place. Under the served weekly clip, the `frequent_bnpl` persona is short on every path even without the purchase (1.000). Under a monthly clip it is 0.653 without and 1.000 with the RM600–799 phone, a visible **+35-point** addition. With a RM1,000 buffer it falls to 0.208 ([engine note N6](../research_notes/PaySense%20hackathon%20edge%20feature/engine_evaluation.md)). These are also exploratory figures. The point is direction, not the exact number: fixing the clip is what lets the Second-Opinion Check say something true and useful about the demo student. The evaluation also flags that this persona's 1.000 baseline may partly come from SPayLater instalment rows confirmed as monthly bills (`questions.md` row 11). That should be checked before anything else.

No new model family is worth adding. The "marginal-impact engine" is the G1 reformulation of the existing LightGBM. The label is rewritten as `p_with = b + (1 − b)·q`, where b is the simulated no-purchase probability and q is the conditional added risk. The model learns q, with reasons computed on q. In the prototype, Brier fell from **0.0127 to 0.0064**, band agreement rose from **0.913 to 0.953**, and added-risk error fell from **0.059 to 0.027** ([engine note G1](../research_notes/PaySense%20hackathon%20edge%20feature/engine_evaluation.md)). Most of that gain comes from using the exact baseline, and the note says so. The scheduler is a search over the existing simulation, not a model. The evaluation rejects four ideas. A loan-stacking ML model would only re-learn the generator, since stacking is already exact in known flows. An ML categoriser would be trained on circular data and would let a model move scores indirectly. An anomaly detector adds little beside the existing MAD rule. A forecast ensemble is a grey area under D-05 and does no better than MA13 in absolute error. Calibration changes, more profiles and relaxed monotone constraints have each been measured as exhausted.

The order is set by dependency. **First**, run the two evaluation-only jobs, which need no ADR or retrain and run in minutes on the cloud's synthetic data. One is the outcome check: cut each development ledger 26 weeks early, simulate, and score P(shortfall) against the realised margins, with constant and MA13 benchmarks. The other reports recursion error against MA13 and naive at 13, 26, 39 and 52 weeks. In the same pass, check the persona data. **Second**, draft the ADRs and get the author's sign-off: F1 and F2 for the band (monthly clip, plus pooled population error paths for short histories, which do produce paths and so do not reopen D-04); G1 and G8 for the target and the counterfactual objective; G4 and G5 for the generator mix and the zero-income savings ratio; G3 for timing; F4 for PTPTN detection. **Third**, do one bundled v3 build: one relabel, one retrain and a **new lockbox**, because the current one has already been scored twice. The note estimates under an hour of compute, which a cloud session can run. Old and new numbers are reported side by side, following the D-50 precedent. **Fourth**, build the scheduler on top of G8's search. **Last**, run the real-data band check on MoneyData and Berka, and the real-data D-06 re-check if the served forecaster changes. Both need the author's machine, because the downloads are blocked in the cloud.

## Build order: one headline, eight supporting changes

The headline ships in two stages, and neither stage invents a number. Stage one is **doable now on cloud**. `POST /api/risk/check` already returns `probability_without_purchase` alongside the shown probability ([`backend/app/api/risk.py`](../../../../backend/app/api/risk.py)), and the generated client carries it. The risk screen already draws the with- and without-purchase cash curves (`frontend/components/risk/RiskResult.tsx`), but no frontend component renders the without-purchase *probability*. A repository search finds it only in tests and `schema.d.ts`. Stage one therefore adds:

- the before and after simulated probabilities and the added points to the risk card and to the assistant's risk tool output, labelled as the simulation, with the Engine 2 band and reasons beside them;
- the cross-provider dues timeline, using existing plan data;
- a correctly labelled line that cites the lender's legal duty and, where the user has entered provider limits, BNM's RM1,500 cumulative-limit trigger;
- a pending action that ends the flow on a confirm card. The existing `create_plan` action can carry the chosen date and tenor; a separate reminder action would need its own tests and a row in the attack suite.

It needs a new ADR recorded under rule 3 for the change to what the card displays, but it does not amend a settled decision. Stage two **needs the author's ADR sign-off**: the G1 target, so the shown number and its reasons describe the purchase; the G8 objective; the scheduler; and the F1/F2 fix, so the demo persona's baseline is not saturated.

## Conclusion

The research changes where PaySense's edge lies. The obvious pitch, "a safe AI assistant", has become the entry ticket: FundBun in Topic A, and Sathi and Guardian in other topics, publish attack scoreboards and confirm-only writes, and judges will mark PaySense down if it does not show the same on screen. The edge is a question only an evaluated forecasting engine can answer honestly: **how much worse this particular purchase makes your next months, across every provider, and when it would not**. Regulators now make lenders ask that question privately, and behavioural evidence says this kind of answer changes behaviour where risk warnings do not.

The less obvious finding is that the headline and the engine work are one project. The current Engine 2 mostly re-learns the student's starting position, and the served simulation saturates the demo persona at certainty, so a "before → after" card built on the current engines would show the weakness instead of the strength. That makes the author's sign-off on the clip fix and the two-part target the single highest-value decision left. One bundled retrain, under an hour of compute, turns an honest "100% → 100%" into a defensible "+35 points, and here is the date it drops". That number should go in the deck only after a committed script regenerates it.
