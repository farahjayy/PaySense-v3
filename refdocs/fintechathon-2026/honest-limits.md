# Honest limits (WP17-C1)

> One paragraph, to be used verbatim in the README, the technical documentation and the deck. Every number comes from a report generated by a script; the sources are listed under the paragraph. Regenerate the paragraph's numbers whenever those reports change. Re-traced for the engine v3 round (Engine 1 band rule R3, Engine 2 v3; ADRs D-60 to D-64); the previous figures are kept in the table as "was".

## The paragraph

PaySense's risk score is trained on **synthetic** students, not on real repayment outcomes: 11,000 generated profiles whose label is the chance, simulated by the forecasting engine, that the student's running margin goes below zero once the plan is added. Those labels are often already decided: **54.4%** are exactly 0 or 1, **42.7%** of students are already short (a 10% or higher chance) before any purchase, and the purchase changes the risk band for only **10.7%**. The model therefore starts from the simulated chance without the purchase and learns only what the purchase adds; its accuracy (lockbox Brier 0.0078, against 0.1608 for always guessing the average and 0.0532 for "the purchase adds nothing") is accuracy at copying the simulation, not at predicting real defaults. The shown score lands in the same band as a direct simulation of the same purchase **95.5%** of the time. When a purchase is not Safe, a smallest change that reaches Safe exists for only **16.0%** of results (17 of 106), because most of those students are short before they buy anything, and for **57%** of them no feature of the purchase stands out as a reason; the app says so instead of inventing a fix. The forecast's 80% band now holds the real 13-week total **80%** of the time for spending and **81%** for income on fresh synthetic students (was 71% and 52%), including the shortest 17–25-week histories (78% and 82%); beyond 13 weeks it narrows again (49% for a 52-week spending total), so long plans are the least reliable. The savings buffer, most generator parameters (115 of 131) and every band check rest on assumptions or synthetic data, not on measured Malaysian student finances.
