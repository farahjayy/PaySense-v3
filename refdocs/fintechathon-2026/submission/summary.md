# Registration summary (draft)

> Target 100–200 words. Figures from `reports/engine1/README.md`, `reports/engine2/versions.md` and `reports/assistant/README.md`; re-check them before submitting.

PaySense helps Malaysian university students decide whether a buy-now-pay-later plan fits before they commit. From a student's own records it forecasts the months ahead (a global LSTM that beat ARIMA, Prophet and benchmarks: MASE 0.82 against 1.04), simulates a thousand futures, and scores a proposed SPayLater, TikTok PayLater or Atome plan as Safe, Caution or At Risk with a calibrated LightGBM model, its reasons, and the smallest change that would make it Safe. Its Second-Opinion Check shows what that one purchase adds to the chance of running short, every instalment already owed across providers, and when buying would add little. An AI assistant answers by calling the app's own tools: it never produces a figure itself (every number it says is checked against the tools' results), and it cannot change anything; it proposes, and the student confirms. In a live evaluation it completed 36 of 36 tasks; an on-screen security drill runs the four named attack classes against a sandbox with a model that obeys every attacker, and no attack does harm. The model is trained on synthetic students, not real repayments, and we say so: its limits are published beside its results. PaySense gives estimates, not financial advice or a credit decision.
